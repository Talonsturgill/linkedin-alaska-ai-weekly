Alaska's Department of Administration has asked vendors to imagine an AI agent that completes whole government transactions inside myAlaska on a resident's behalf. The coverage read it as a capability arriving. It isn't one yet.

In its November 25, 2025 RFI 02-107-26 for the myAlaska Mobile Application Enhancement, the DOA solicited interest in agentic AI capable of the “autonomous completion of multi-step government service transactions on behalf of users.” That is myAlaska, the portal Alaskans use for PFD applications, professional licensing, and unemployment. GovTech, the Anchorage Daily News, Biometric Update, and Alaska Watchman all picked it up. Five independent outlets is a real signal of state intent.

So the read is reasonable. If a state formally asks the market for autonomous agentic government services, vendors and budget planners have grounds to treat that trajectory as near-term and to start positioning. The capability is technically conceivable, and the document is genuine.

Here's where it breaks. No US state has put production agentic AI into government services at this scope. NASCIO, the national association of state CIOs, in its March 2026 report “Beyond Generation,” frames agentic AI as something states still have to anticipate, build guardrails for, and build trust around before deployment. NASCIO doesn't name Alaska, but the immaturity it describes is sector-wide, and Alaska shares it. And the document the DOA issued is an RFI, a request for information soliciting feasibility input, not an RFP and not an award. According to GovTech, the DOA did not respond to questions about the project. The coverage conflated a market-sounding with a near-ready system.

The load-bearing distinction is the one the headlines skipped.

- An RFI asks what is possible.
- An RFP commits to build it.
- No state has shipped this capability into production.

That gap is where the Alaska business risk sits. AI vendors and systems integrators are reportedly shaping Alaska-specific bid strategies around a premise (autonomous agentic state transactions at scale) that has no proven government deployment model anywhere in the US. If the RFI framing carries straight into an eventual RFP, it imports unrealistic timelines and liability expectations. Budget and service-continuity risk then lands on the DOA, and on residents who depend on myAlaska for the PFD, for licenses, and for unemployment when they need it most.

Alaska's IT modernization story is running ahead of the maturity curve the whole sector is on. That's worth knowing before anyone prices a bid.

When a state RFI describes a capability no state has deployed, should integrators bid the vision or bid the proven floor?
