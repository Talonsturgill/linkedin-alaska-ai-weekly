A V-BAT vertical-takeoff drone flew live surveillance off the Coast Guard cutter Waesche this month, the first operational integration of that unmanned system on a national security cutter in Alaskan waters. Hardware in the water, not a slide deck.

The Waesche (WMSL 751) returned from a 113-day Bering Sea patrol of roughly 18,685 nautical miles, and per Janes it flew persistent ISR off its own deck the whole way, plus the first-ever fueling-at-sea with the new polar icebreaker USCGC Storis at Dutch Harbor. Vice Adm. Nathan Moore said in April that Chinese and Russian vessel activity near Alaska is rising. Read those together and the signal is clear. Federal demand for autonomous capability over Alaska’s fisheries enforcement zone and Arctic approaches is now operational and recurring, not theoretical.

The same week showed how thin the rails under that demand are. On May 27 a GCI subsea fiber cable severed between Larsen Bay and Chignik Bay, cutting internet and wireless to six communities (Akutan, Chignik Bay, False Pass, King Cove, Perryville, and Unalaska). Repair-vessel transit runs 7 to 14 days, with mobile voice and texting partly restored on backup satellite. It was the second Aleutian subsea break in roughly six months. Autonomous ISR and edge AI assume a connectivity substrate. In the Aleutians that substrate is a single line that snaps twice a year.

The workforce rail runs on its own clock. NSF’s joint program with the Labor Department, TechAccess AI-Ready America (NSF 26-508), funds about $1 million a year for three years per state for an AI workforce coordination hub. Round 1 Letters of Intent are due June 16, full proposals July 16, and only 10 hubs get picked in Round 1. The obvious applicant is the University of Alaska system. The desk’s read is that missing the first cohort cedes early position to states that filed.

Here’s the question under all three. Does Alaska capture this work or just host it for outside firms? It can capture. Tuknik Government Services, a Koniag subsidiary, recently won the 2026 ACT-IAC Innovation Impact Award for an Army agentic-AI suite the announcement says saves more than $37 million and 687,000 work hours a year. That proves an Alaska Native Corporation can deliver federal AI at award-winning scale. The open question is whether that’s the exception or the onramp.

Three rails, three clocks. Connectivity, workforce, contracting. Which one breaks Alaska’s chance to capture this buildout first, and who should be the one to fix it?
