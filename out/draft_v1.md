On Sept. 15, Anchorage Mayor Suzanne LaFrance introduced her own version of the city’s police surveillance ordinance, and the Assembly voted unanimously to push the fight to Oct. 20. Meanwhile the AI-enabled crime center keeps running.

LaFrance is the elected chief executive of the Municipality of Anchorage, so APD’s real-time crime center, license plate readers, and live camera monitoring all run under her administration.

Per the Assembly’s legislative record, after the continued public hearing on AO 2026-108 closed, she introduced an administration substitute, AO 2026-108(S), to replace the Assembly sponsors’ ordinance. The sponsors’ version sets hard statutory limits on data retention, ALPR use, and external data networks, and it requires Assembly pre-approval for acquisitions. Her substitute would instead codify APD’s existing policies, with reporting and oversight provisions. According to the Anchorage Daily News, she warned the sponsors’ restrictions could “unduly limit some core functions of APD operations,” including misdemeanor enforcement and joint state and federal drug work. The Assembly postponed with her version (S) and the sponsors’ version (S-1) still unreconciled.

The choice is binary. Either the city writes down what APD already does, or it writes hard limits around what APD can do.

My read is that her operational concern is legitimate. A mayor accountable for misdemeanor enforcement and joint drug cases should ask whether blunt retention caps break working investigations, and her substitute isn’t empty, since it adds oversight and public reporting. But codifying current practice isn’t the same as constraining it. Reportedly more than $12M has already been directed to the crime center, plus a roughly $600K equipment contract. I’d expect the guardrails to come before money at that scale, not after. The retention caps, the external-network ban, and Assembly pre-approval decide who holds the data and who approves the next purchase. All three are still unadopted. A competing executive text plus a postponement means the apparatus runs on current practice while the stronger rules wait.

The next decision point is Oct. 20. The Assembly takes the postponed matter off Unfinished Business and has to reconcile (S) against (S-1). Watch three things. Does the administration version prevail? Do the hard limits on retention, ALPRs, external networks, and acquisitions survive in any form? And how does the final text govern the crime center’s AI use?

Should Anchorage write retention caps and Assembly pre-approval into the ordinance before the crime center scales further, or codify current APD practice first and tighten it later?
