On June 11 the SBA published a proposed rule that rewrites who qualifies as socially disadvantaged for individual 8(a) contracting. The comment window closes July 13. The dollars at stake in Alaska run into the billions.

Federal officials are saying the part Alaska wants to hear. Alaska Native Corporations, tribes, and Native Hawaiian Organizations are explicitly exempt. The rule touches individual applicants, not entity-owned firms. DoD Under Secretary Michael Duffey told Alaska Public Media on June 12 that ANC participation is grounded in their political status as Native Americans, not in DEI. The Alaska Legislature passed HJR 44 in bipartisan support of ANC 8(a) participation, and Rep. Maxine Dibert and Sen. Cathy Giessel made the same case in a June 15 op-ed. On paper, the exemption holds.

Here is the structural problem the paper exemption doesn’t solve. The rule doesn’t shrink the set-aside. It enlarges and re-credentials the individual applicant pool competing for the same pot. In FY2024 Native-owned firms took roughly $16.1B of about $26B in 8(a) awards, per Tribal Business News. ANC federal-contracting subsidiaries in IT, AI, cloud, cybersecurity, and defense are built on that channel. And ANC contract obligations were already reportedly down sharply, around 46 percent October through April, per a May 11 Fortune report. An exemption protects eligibility. It does not protect market share when more credentialed individual firms chase the same awards over a five-year recompete horizon.

That recompete horizon is the real clock, and it isn’t the only one. The federal contracting environment ANCs depend on is being re-plumbed on two fronts at once.

- 8(a) eligibility, where the SBA rule reshapes the competitor pool by July 13.
- AI procurement, where NSPM-11 (June 5) orders a 120-day overhaul and multi-vendor onboarding due around October 3.
- Cyber compliance, where NSPM-12 (June 12) hands the NSA Director binding directive authority over National Security Systems operators.

None of this is abstract. The AI work is physically landing in Alaska right now. XPRIZE Wildfire runs its $11M finals June 15 through 25 across a 1,000 square kilometer rural Alaska test zone, with UAF’s ACUASI as test partner and Anduril among five teams racing to autonomously suppress an ignition in under ten minutes. Alaska’s $272M federal Rural Health grant entered an AI-heavy shortlist with a June 22 supplemental deadline. Rep. Genevieve Mina said the program design is “almost directing us to AI.”

The question is whether Alaska firms own that economy or merely host it. The set-aside is the instrument that decides which.

So here is the debate. Does a paper exemption on entity eligibility actually defend ANC market share once the individual pool gets larger and better-credentialed, or does Alaska need to fight the recompete math directly before July 13?
