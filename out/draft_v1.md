Alaska’s legislature handed Governor Dunleavy its first explicit AI law on June 1 with an 18-day clock that runs out June 18. In the same window, Washington is moving to strip the state of the authority to write AI law at all.

The bill is HB 239. The Senate passed it 20-0 on May 19 and the House concurred 39-1 on May 20, the last day of the regular session, per the akleg.gov record and Alaska Public Media. It is an omnibus crime bill, and folded inside it is the state’s first criminalization of AI-generated child sexual abuse material. The headline is the content. The structural fact is the authority. Alaska just legislated AI conduct, and the question is whether it gets to keep doing so.

The conventional framing out of Washington is that one national AI standard beats fifty state ones. Per Roll Call’s March 20 background, the White House AI framework calls for preemption of state AI laws. That reads clean. It is the false-clean option. Preemption does not produce a careful federal rulebook for Alaska workplaces. It deletes Juneau’s seat and hands the rules for AI hiring, scheduling, and monitoring to a framework Alaska did not write and cannot amend.

This is not abstract, because AI is already operating inside the state’s largest private workforce. Per the AFA Alaska Scheduling Committee recap of May 29, Alaska Airlines management introduced a vendor called AI Crew Solutions to automate vacation slot bidding for flight attendants, with user-acceptance testing underway. On May 31, LeiLauni Scheideman, president of AFA Council 30 in Anchorage, published an ADN op-ed urging Congress not to preempt state authority over AI in employment. She cites that roughly 90% of employers now use AI to filter and rank applicants, and references Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s warning that AI could replace half of entry-level jobs within five years.

The pressure runs underneath all of it. Alaska’s minimum wage steps from $13 to $14 on July 1 under Ballot Measure 1, with $15 to follow in 2027. Read that as automation ROI math for service employers, not a prediction.

The broader in-window footprint is wider than one airline.

- DOE opened a $3.5M C-MAP microgrid round, applications due July 2, with Alaska Native villages and corporations eligible.
- XPRIZE’s $11M autonomous-wildfire AI finals are scheduled in the NANA region near Fairbanks, finalists reportedly including Anduril and Dryad.
- HB 239 sits on the governor’s desk while a vendor automates union scheduling in Anchorage.

If preemption lands while HB 239 is still in transit, Alaska loses authority it is exercising this week. Two clocks, opposite directions, decided now.
