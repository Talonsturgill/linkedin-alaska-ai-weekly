On June 22 the Federal Register published a one-year renewal that almost nobody in the Alaska data center conversation read. NMFS authorized HEX Operating to incidentally harass marine mammals while towing a production rig in Cook Inlet, and that single permit quietly gates Southcentral’s power supply.

Under MMPA Section 101(a)(5)(D), one signature inside NMFS decides whether a Cook Inlet gas operator can run its production season at all. Here is how the machinery stacks up.

- MMPA statutory authority, which requires federal permission before any non-fishing activity may incidentally take marine mammals, enacted by Congress and administered by NMFS.
- The applicant layer, where HEX Operating LLC (formerly Furie) must apply every year to relocate the Enterprise 151 jack-up rig to the Allegra Lee Platform, controlled by HEX itself.
- The ESA Section 7 biological opinion, which must clear the activity against Cook Inlet beluga jeopardy before any authorization issues, controlled by the NMFS Alaska Regional Office.
- The OPR Director’s signature, the single document that legally permits the production season, controlled by Kimberly Damon-Randall, Director of the NMFS Office of Protected Resources.
- Cook Inlet gas feeding the gas-fired Railbelt grid that powers every proposed Southcentral data center, controlled by Chugach Electric and the other Railbelt utilities.

The leverage sits entirely in that fourth layer. Damon-Randall signed the renewal on June 16, effective September 13, 2026 through September 12, 2027. The binary she owns is issue versus deny or delay. There’s no committee override, no co-signer, and no substitute pathway under MMPA for this class of activity. Without her signature, HEX can’t tow the rig and can’t produce gas for the season.

This is the upstream permit nobody connects to the buildout, and that’s exactly why it matters. Every proposed AI data center in the Anchorage, Kenai, and Mat-Su corridor runs on Railbelt electricity, which is majority gas-fired on Cook Inlet gas, whose production is gated annually by one federal signature. The Alaska data center debate fixates on land leases and interconnection queues. It skips the permit that decides whether there’s gas to burn. Chugach’s June 2026 letter of intent with Glenfarne for future Alaska LNG pipeline gas reads as the utilities admitting this annual NMFS gate is a vulnerability they can’t control.

So the developers chasing Southcentral sites should be asking Chugach, in writing, whether their power supply contract depends on Cook Inlet gas from platforms that need an MMPA renewal every year, and whether the September 13 effective date opens a Q4 2026 supply gap that hits construction-phase procurement.

If one OPR Director’s annual signature can gate a region’s gas-fired power, should Southcentral data center power contracts be allowed to rest on it at all?
