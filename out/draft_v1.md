BLM opened the federal right-of-way renewal docket for the Trans Alaska Pipeline on Aug. 26, and scoping closes Sept. 10. Federal law already settled the renewal. The only live decision is the terms.

Under 30 U.S.C. 185, the government must renew the TAPS corridor grant so long as the pipeline keeps operating. One BLM office is writing thirty years of corridor conditions on a fifteen-day window.

- The renewal floor, 30 U.S.C. 185(n), caps the grant at thirty years and removes any discretion to refuse renewal while the line operates, held by the Secretary of the Interior.
- The conditioning power, 185(f), lets the same office prescribe terms over extent, duration, location, construction, operation, maintenance, use and termination, with no limiting standard.
- The renewal docket, NEPA Number DOI-BLM-AK-9410-2026-0009-EA, runs scoping and drafts the stipulations package, controlled by BLM Alaska State Director Kevin Pendergast.
- The grantee stack, Alyeska Pipeline Service Company applying for owners Harvest Alaska LLC, ConocoPhillips Transportation Alaska Inc. and ExxonMobil Pipeline Company LLC.
- The parallel state gate, lease ADL 63574 under AS 38.35.070, is adding about 26.1 acres of riprap stockpile ground at mileposts 77.5 through 82.4, decided by State Pipeline Coordinator Anthony Strupulis.

The leverage sits in the NEPA instrument, and Pendergast owns the binary. Build the thirty-year record on the posted path, an environmental assessment carried in the -EA suffix of BLM’s own NEPA number and reported by Alaska Public Media as a lighter review than an EIS, or elevate it. No committee vote, no rate case, no legislative concurrence.

A thirty-year land-use rulebook gets written while looking like a routine permit. Congress guaranteed the renewal in 185(n) and left every term unguarded in 185(f). A thinner record supports a thinner stipulations package, and a thinner package is easier to issue and harder to contest or renegotiate for thirty years. The previous renewal was built on an environmental impact statement, which is why the instrument choice is the story.

The loud question, whether TAPS gets renewed, is already answered. The quiet one, what conditions attach to corridor use, allocates value for a generation on the state’s only continuous North Slope to tidewater industrial corridor, and it is being settled with almost nobody at the table.

A scoping comment on DOI-BLM-AK-9410-2026-0009-EA by Sept. 10 is the cheapest action available, because it’s the only step that builds a record before the stipulations exist. After that you’re arguing against a draft instead of shaping one. Objections at ADL 63574 close 5 p.m. Sept. 21. Alyeska and its three owners are positioned in both forums as the applicant. Dalton Highway and North Slope developers needing access roads, gravel, water or crossings are in neither unless they file this week.

Should one state director pick the NEPA instrument that fixes corridor terms for thirty years, or does a grant this consequential warrant an EIS by default?
