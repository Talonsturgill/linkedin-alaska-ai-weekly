NTIA reopened BEAD on September 3 with a supplemental deployment policy notice, a round aimed at locations the first pass missed. Sen. Dan Sullivan says it could make over 5,000 Alaska locations eligible.

Roll Call reported on September 4 that Commerce will accept new state proposals targeting “newly identified unserved locations,” those made eligible by FCC map changes or program defaults, and that NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth visited Alaska for a roundtable with broadband providers.

Plainly stated, it’s a savings true-up. States that underspent in the first round can redeploy unspent BEAD funds to newly eligible locations, on a re-bid then re-approve cycle NTIA must sign off on before a dollar is drawn.

Three layers carry it.

- FCC Broadband Serviceable Location Fabric and National Broadband Map, whose biannual updates create the “newly identified unserved locations,” controlled by the Commission under 47 U.S.C. 642.
- NTIA BEAD approval and reallocation authority, which approves or disapproves each state’s supplemental final proposal and can reallocate unspent savings, controlled by the Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Communications and Information under 47 U.S.C. 1702.
- Subgrantee deployment in rural Alaska, the last and middle mile build itself, controlled by GCI Holdings, whose parent GCI Liberty’s FY2025 10-K conditions rural construction on BEAD subsidy availability.

Leverage sits in the middle layer. Under 47 U.S.C. 1702(c)(5)(C) and (g)(3), one statutory officer approves, rejects, or reallocates Alaska’s supplemental proposal and the unspent savings behind it, an estimated $370 million. The FCC and the state broadband office are upstream. The money gate is a single desk, not a committee.

The substrate is what’s actually being decided. Last and middle mile connectivity to newly unserved rural Alaska is the physical floor under any AI workload outside the Railbelt. Edge compute, satellite and remote sensing pipelines, and telehealth or resource monitoring inference all stop where fiber and fixed wireless stop. Redeploying those savings to thousands of Bush locations is structurally a decision about where Alaska AI can be deployed at all, and that authority persists no matter who holds the office.

If you allocate to or operate rural connectivity or edge compute, map exposure to GCI Holdings’ BEAD-dependent rural builds and treat the Administrator’s approval as the binary gating that capital. Submissions were triggered by the September 3 notice and a decision is expected this fall. Confirm which locations the state broadband office intends to include before the window closes, rather than assuming first-round awards define the footprint.

Should approve, reject, and reallocate authority over a state’s unspent BEAD savings sit with one federal officer, or does a build this remote need a gate that can’t close on a single signature?
