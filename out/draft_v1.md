A Koniag subsidiary just booked the one unambiguous AI contract any Alaska entity landed this month. Roughly $1.2 million from the Air Force Research Laboratory to build a model that flags which airmen are about to quit.

Per a federal-award tracker, Koniag Technology Solutions took the award on June 22 (ID FA701426C0017), an 18-month sole-source small-business set-aside to deploy an AI/ML model that scores attrition risk and recommends retention interventions. Koniag is an Alaska Native Corporation. Read alongside Chenega Services and Federal Solutions, another ANC subsidiary, winning a roughly $2.4 million CDC information-security contract on June 18, and the pattern is clear. Two ANCs on the federal sheet in one week.

The conventional read says this is Alaska finally catching the defense-AI wave. The Senate Armed Services Committee advanced the FY2027 NDAA on June 23, 18 to 9, carrying 56 Sullivan provisions worth about $2.6 billion for Alaska per his office, including an Analytics and Simulation Laboratory at the Ted Stevens Center and $10 million for autonomous systems inside Golden Dome. Worth saying plainly, that is a committee-advanced authorization, not money appropriated. The floor vote and conference are still pending.

The structural read is narrower. The federal AI dollar is real and growing, but the Alaska entities actually built to capture it are a thin set, and they are ANCs. Back in Vol. 01 the desk argued the gap was never engineers, it was the compliance and contract-vehicle file. The ANCs solved that file years ago through 8(a). Now the file itself is moving. On June 11 the SBA proposed dropping race as a standalone basis for individual 8(a) eligibility, with ANCs, tribes, and Native Hawaiian Organizations explicitly carved out. So the vehicle that built Alaska’s only fluent federal capturers is being reshaped while the dollars sit on the table.

Two more gates are forming at once. Both NDAA drafts direct DoD to stand up a department-wide system for deploying agentic AI, and the Senate version adds a CMMC Level 2 grant program for small and nontraditional contractors. That is the next compliance gate, and it favors firms that already own the back office. Meanwhile Alaska’s federal civilian workforce is thinning. The state Department of Labor’s June 18 release shows roughly 1,500 federal civilian jobs gone over twelve months, about 10% of that workforce.

So the capture window is open, the gates are tightening, and the proven Alaska capturers are a handful of ANCs. If the rest of the industrial base doesn’t get fluent before agentic-AI and CMMC Level 2 harden into the new gate, who in Alaska beyond the ANCs actually banks the next federal AI award?
