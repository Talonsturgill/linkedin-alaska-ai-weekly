Alaska’s Department of Health is preparing Year 2 of the Rural Health Transformation Program “even as the state has yet to finish distributing the first round,” ADN reported. The lag shows who decides where $272 million goes.

The mechanism is a pass-through. Congress funds a $50 billion program, CMS allots it to states against plans that must “prioritize data and technology driven solutions,” and Alaska routes its $272,174,856 Year 1 award through one discretionary office.

- Statute, SSA 2105(h) appropriates $10 billion a year through FY2030 with a tech-forward plan mandate, fixed by Congress
- Federal allotment, CMS approves plans and can withhold, reduce, or recover payments, held by the CMS Administrator
- State award authority, DOH makes every funding call, held by Commissioner Heidi Hedberg
- Subaward intake, the Alaska Community Foundation runs the portal and manages subawards under contract to DOH
- Year 2 targeting, open calls give way to “state-directed targeted innovation funding opportunities,” set by the Commissioner

Layer three is the chokepoint. DOH states that “the DOH Commissioner retains final decision-making authority on all funding decisions.” The binary is fund or don’t fund, plus the pen on Year 2 criteria. The RHTP Advisory Council only advises. The one upstream check is the CMS Administrator’s clawback.

Congress wrote a technology tilt into the statute. Rep. Genevieve Mina has said the federal design is “almost directing us to AI,” according to GovTech. In Alaska that tilt passes through one office before it reaches anyone. Year 1 went out as stabilization in weekly batches. Year 2 narrows further, into three focus areas ADN reports as pay-for-value reimbursement, care coordination, and emergency medical services. None is labeled technology.

That’s a sensible design for Alaska’s provider map, and it gives CMS one accountable office. But it means the money is real for Alaska AI and health-IT vendors without being addressed to them. It arrives through providers. Foundation Health Partners’ $16 million award for data and technology to better focus on prevention, per Alaska’s News Source, is the template. The provider holds the award and the technology rides inside a care project.

The window is short. ADN reports Year 2 criteria comments close Oct. 9 and remaining Year 1 funds release at the end of October. DOH says new applications could open “as early as fall 2026,” and Year 1 money must be fully spent by Sept. 30, 2027. Vendors who comment now and pair with a hospital, tribal health organization, or EMS agency are positioned. Standalone vendor applications aren’t.

Should Alaska health-tech firms ask the Commissioner to name technology as an explicit Year 2 criterion by Oct. 9, or build it inside provider projects and let the categories stand?
