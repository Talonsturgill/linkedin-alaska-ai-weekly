RED FLAG-Alaska 26-2 closed June 12 with drone reconnaissance folded into a live multinational forcible entry at Donnelly Drop Zone. Over 2,100 troops and 70 aircraft from five nations ran it across JPARC. Alaska wasn’t the backdrop. It was the test.

The 1-40th Cavalry of the 11th Airborne integrated uncrewed reconnaissance with live TOW and mortar fires in a contested environment during the exercise, which concluded June 12. That matters past the headline. JPARC is functioning as the Defense Department’s cold-environment proving ground for uncrewed-systems doctrine, and the doctrine and requirements written on that range become the procurement spec UAS vendors will have to meet. Whoever sets the standard at the edge of what works in the Arctic ends up writing the buy. The leverage of being the gatekeeper is real. The risk is that the gatekeeper furnishes the R&D while the program offices and recurring revenue sit with firms headquartered Outside.

The same pattern runs through compute. The Department of the Air Force has an open RFLP for privately financed and operated AI data centers on parcels across three installations at JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS, with proposals due June 29. In-window reporting documented Interior pushback over permafrost, noise, water, and Golden Valley Electric Association grid-cost exposure, with one parcel reportedly under a mile from North Pole Middle School. The RFLP makes the developer secure its own power, yet residents fear the costs land on the backs of GVEA members, and no Alaska ratepayer-protection statute is in place. This is the live test of whether AI compute can run on permafrost and a strained Interior grid, and of who pays when it does.

Connectivity is proving out the same way. GCI announced June 11 it is deploying Starlink dedicated bonded gateways at Bethel, Sitka, Kotzebue, and Dillingham for multi-gigabit middle-mile capacity, extending its 2024 SpaceX reseller agreement after installs at Unalaska and Nome. The substrate works. It works on SpaceX’s platform, with GCI reselling.

So the stakes are about who keeps what. The North Slope is being floated for the same role, with the Stak Energy private AI campus reported in late May and a comment period expected to close in mid-June. Alaska is fast becoming the country’s hardest test bed for autonomy, AI compute, and remote connectivity. The open question is whether being the proving ground converts into Alaska-held programs, or just into free validation for vendors who bank the contract elsewhere.

If Alaska is going to prove this technology works, what should it demand in return before the next RED FLAG or RFLP cycle locks the terms?
