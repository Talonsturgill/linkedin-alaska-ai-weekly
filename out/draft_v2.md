Bering Straits Native Corporation just posted a record dividend. The ANC 8(a) sole-source pipeline that produced it has been frozen at a single administrative gate since August 2025.

Haven Harris, SVP Growth and Strategy at Bering Straits Native Corporation, told Alaska Public Media on May 14 that both dividends came from federal contracting access. It has four layers.

Four layers drive this mechanism from statute to execution.

- Federal Statutory Foundation (U.S. Congress and Sen. Lisa Murkowski), establishes 8(a) eligibility for ANCs under 15 U.S.C. § 637 with no sole-source dollar ceiling, a structural advantage no other class of 8(a) participant holds.
- SBA Administrator Eligibility Gate (Kelly Loeffler, Senate-confirmed), holds non-delegable authority to approve or deny 8(a) program participation. No new applications have cleared since August 2025.
- ANC Subsidiary Corporate Structure (individual ANC boards at Chugach Alaska Corporation, Doyon, Bering Straits Native Corporation, and Koniag, Inc.), where separate 8(a)-certified operating subsidiaries hold the actual federal contracts and generate the dividends.
- Alaska State Legislative Backstop (Alaska Legislature and Nicole Borromeo, ANCSA Regional Association), HJR 44 passed 40-0 in the House on April 29 and 20-0 in the Senate on May 11, requesting federal preservation of ANC 8(a) participation, but it does not legally bind the SBA.

The chokepoint is the SBA Administrator Eligibility and Application Gate. Kelly Loeffler's eligibility determination is non-delegable under the statute. No court order, no state resolution, and no congressional mandate short of statutory amendment can force the Administrator to process applications on any particular timeline. Roughly 600 applications cleared in 2024. Zero since August 2025. One Senate-confirmed officer controls that valve.

The freeze isn't a legal abolition of the program. Existing certified firms, including Arctic IT under Doyon and Chugach Government Solutions, continue receiving awards. But the subsidiary pipeline shrinks through attrition every month new certifications don't clear. Federal AI spending is accelerating right now, and 8(a)-certified firms hold the lowest-barrier entry into that spending wave. ANC-owned contractors in Alaska are losing competitive ground to non-ANC firms during the window when ground matters most.

ANC boards should audit expiring 8(a) certifications now and accelerate bids under current credentials before attrition narrows the pool further. The only lever that doesn't require the Administrator's voluntary cooperation is a statutory fix or appropriations rider through Sen. Murkowski's office that imposes processing timelines on the SBA.

If the freeze extends into late 2026, which Alaska AI and technology firm is best positioned to hold ground without new 8(a) certifications, and which faces the sharpest attrition risk?
