Governor Dunleavy told Data Center World that AI developers siting in Alaska might see four or five cents a kilowatt hour for decades to come. If your model uses that number, your operating budget is off by tens of millions.

The pitch, reported by Data Center Knowledge on 7 October 2025, is worth taking seriously. Per Data Center Knowledge, the Governor said Alaska hopes to deliver gas to Southcentral in two years, and that data farms could then be, in his words, “looking at four or five cents a kilowatt hour for decades and decades to come.” The mechanism is real. Stranded gas plus a captive state-backed power purchase agreement is exactly how Norway, Iceland, and Quebec landed below-market industrial power for energy-hungry tenants. Fuel-indexed PPAs are a proven tool, and Alaska’s stranded gas is genuinely large.

So the claim is plausible. It’s just not a price anyone can buy today.

Here’s where a CFO has to be careful. The four or five cents is a delivered price for an unbuilt pipeline, tied to Alaska LNG, a project studied for decades without breaking ground. The Governor’s own words give it away. “We hope to be delivering gas” in two years is a contingency, not a rate sheet. The price a developer can actually contract in Alaska right now sits much higher. EIA’s Electric Power Monthly (Table 5.6.A, March 2026) puts Alaska commercial power at 23.02 cents per kWh and industrial at 25.32 cents, roughly five to six times the figure quoted. EIA’s state profile puts the all-sector average at 22.17 cents, the sixth highest in the nation.

The supply side reinforces the gap. Chugach Electric, the utility serving Southcentral, said in May 2026 through spokesperson Julie Hasquet that it currently does not have gas to serve a large data center. Its Hilcorp supply contract ends in 2028 and Cook Inlet gas is tightening.

The accurate frame for an underwriting model is short.

- Underwrite today’s grid at 23 to 25 cents, not a future PPA.
- Treat the four to five cent number as an upside case contingent on Alaska LNG financing and first gas.
- Price Cook Inlet supply risk into any Southcentral siting decision before 2028.

The stakes land on real decisions. Any hyperscaler or AI developer weighing Alaska in the next 12 to 24 months, a DigitalBridge, a Stak Energy, an Air Force Enhanced Use Lease bidder, that pencils in four or five cents will understate opex by tens of millions per year for every 100 MW of load. Power price is the single largest variable in a siting go or no-go, and getting it wrong kills deals after the capital is committed.

If you were modeling an Alaska data center today, what discount rate would you put on a power price contingent on a pipeline that hasn’t broken ground?
