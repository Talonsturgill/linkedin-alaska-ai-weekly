Alaska DNR opened a 60-day public comment window on May 12 for a $500 million, one-square-mile, 3-gigawatt AI data center on the North Slope. Comments close June 15. The statute book that would have priced it just died in Juneau.

Anchorage-based Stak Energy is the proposer, financed by McKinley Alaska Private Investment, sited about 25 miles south of major North Slope infrastructure off the Dalton Highway. Per the preliminary best-interest determination, DNR is prepared to grant a 50-year land lease. Initial operations are targeted for 2028. The project would draw up to 3 GW from on-site natural gas generation, more than twice the gas urban Alaska burns for electricity and heat combined. No firm gas supply contract has been disclosed, and turbine supply chains are reportedly backlogged seven years.

The conventional framing is that this is a siting story. Land, gas, tax base, jobs. That framing is structurally incomplete. The binding constraint isn’t the lease, it’s the absence of the rules that would have governed who pays for what and who gets what in return. SB 250 would have required data centers to bear their own utility infrastructure costs and to enter community benefit agreements approved by the Regulatory Commission of Alaska. The Senate passed it on reconsideration 14-5 on May 16. The House Community and Regional Affairs committee heard-and-held it on May 18. It died at sine die on May 21.

Read against the rest of the week, the pattern sharpens.

- Stak’s 2028 in-service target sits a year ahead of Glenfarne’s 2029 first-gas target, even after the May 18 ConocoPhillips 30-year precedent agreement brought Glenfarne’s supply roster to four (ExxonMobil, Hilcorp, Great Bear Pantheon, ConocoPhillips).
- Santos and Repsol announced Pikka first oil on May 18 with a designed-in remote operations center at camp, the staffing model the next wave of North Slope operators is designing toward.
- The HB 78 pension veto override fell on May 19 at 33 of 60 votes, seven short of the 40 required. That is the recruiting math state agencies and federal contractors now have to staff this build with.

The third option nobody is naming is sequencing. RCA-approved cost allocation and community benefit terms can be written as a condition of the DNR lease itself, not deferred to a future session that may or may not pass a replacement for SB 250. The comment window closes June 15. After that, the lease terms harden, and the precedent for the next gigawatt-scale proposal hardens with them. Capital is moving on a 2028 clock. Juneau is on a biennial one. Whichever clock wins this round sets the rules for the decade.

Should DNR fold cost-allocation and community-benefit terms into the lease itself, or wait for a statute that didn’t survive this session?
