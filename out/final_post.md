Governor Dunleavy is telling data center developers Alaska will deliver “four or five cents a kilowatt hour for decades and decades to come.” Pro formas are being underwritten on that number right now.

The quote comes from Mike Dunleavy at Data Center World in October 2025, reported by Data Center Knowledge, and repeated at Data Center World again in April 2026 per Broadband Breakfast. The State’s preliminary “best interest” finding for Stak Energy’s 50-year North Slope lease for a $500M, 3GW data center rests on the same number.

Smart people believe it for real reasons. Alaska has enormous proven stranded gas. If Alaska LNG ever reaches Final Investment Decision, Southcentral industrial power could in theory fall toward single digits. Hyperscalers underwrite on 10-to-20-year horizons. That’s the steelman, and it’s honest.

It breaks against today’s primary sources. Chugach Electric spokesperson Julie Hasquet told ADN on May 5, 2026 that the utility “currently does not have gas to serve a large data center.” Large commercial users on Chugach pay roughly 14 to 17 cents per kilowatt hour today. The Air Force’s own April 2026 Request for Lease Proposals says JBER sites have “insufficient power” and will need a new connection to that same Chugach grid. Hilcorp is raising gas prices to Mat-Su utilities by 49% to $11.75 per Mcf by 2028, and Enstar President John Sims called it “the start of an economy that just doesn’t work anymore.” Alaska LNG, the pipeline that would deliver the Governor’s headline rate, has no FID and at least a $44 billion cost estimate. Wood Mackenzie analysis cited by Stan Jones projects Phase 1 gas at $10 to $13.70 per MMBtu against today’s roughly $8.69. The pipeline’s gas would be more expensive than the gas already being called unaffordable.

Four lines from the primary record.

- Alaska industrial power today is 14 to 17 cents per kWh versus Virginia 8.99, Texas 6.12, Washington 6.61 (EIA 2024).
- The serving utility is on record with no gas for a large new customer.
- The Air Force’s chosen bases (JBER, Eielson, Clear) need new connections to that same utility.
- Alaska LNG has no FID, and Senate Majority Leader Cathy Giessel called Glenfarne’s timeline “completely unrealistic.”

The stake is the Air Force’s April 2026 RLP. Offers were due May 29, 2026. Anyone bidding a JBER, Eielson, or Clear site on a 4-to-5-cent assumption is pricing a future state that doesn’t have a pipeline, a FID, or a serving utility yet. Robert Pickett, a former RCA Commissioner, told ADN on May 5 that “the whole electric industry right now in Southcentral Alaska is in a world of hurt.”

If you were underwriting an Alaska data center site this quarter, what’s the defensible blended power rate, and over what horizon?
