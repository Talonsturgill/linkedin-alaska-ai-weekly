The Air Force wants privately financed AI data centers on Alaska bases under leases running up to 50 years, proposals due June 29. Read RFLP AFCEC-26-R-0006-O closely and the seat Alaska is being offered is landlord, not operator.

Per the June 5 solicitation, the Air Force Civil Engineer Center is offering parcels across three installations, JBER, Eielson, and Clear Space Force Station, for data centers a private developer finances, builds, and runs. Alaska and its firms collect land rent, construction work, and grid offtake. The AI workloads and the recurring contract revenue belong to whoever operates the buildings. Residents near Eielson have pushed back since June 8 over permafrost, noise, water, and Golden Valley Electric Association load, with one off-base parcel reportedly sitting less than a mile from North Pole Middle School.

Here's the structural trap. The landlord seat looks safe because it always pays. But rent is a one-time-ish capture and offtake is a commodity. The operator holds the workloads, the data, and the recompete advantage that compounds for decades. Banking on land and preference means cashing out the lowest-margin layer of a 50-year asset.

And the operator seat is widening right now. NSPM-11, signed June 5, directs DoD and the intelligence community to break AI vendor lock-in, bring in more and smaller commercial vendors, update the autonomy directive DoDD 3000.09 within 90 days, and reset AI procurement inside roughly 120 days. The memo doesn't name Alaska. But the defense industrial base at JBER, Eielson, Clear, and Fort Wainwright is exactly the kind of operator pool federal buyers are reportedly being told to grow.

The exception proves the point. On June 11 the SBA proposed ending automatic race-based 8(a) eligibility for individual firms while carving out Alaska Native Corporation entity-owned firms, Koniag, Chenega, NANA, Sealaska, Doyon. The preference lane Alaska reliably wins gets more durable for ANCs even as it narrows for everyone else. That's a real edge. It's also still a preference, the opposite vector from a procurement system trying to widen who competes on merit.

Three federal clocks now run at once.

- June 29, DAF lease proposals due.
- Around July 13, SBA comment period closes.
- Around October, the NSPM-11 procurement reset lands.

The power math stays unsolved underneath all of it. A June 4 grant reported at $89M went to Terra Energy Center for a 1,250-megawatt coal-with-carbon-capture plant west of Skwentna, framed as data-center power, with no permitted mine, no design, and no signed offtake. The landlord model needs power it can't yet pencil.

So which seat should Alaska fight for before June 29, the contingent landlord seat federal leases hand over, or the operator seat NSPM-11 is prying open?
