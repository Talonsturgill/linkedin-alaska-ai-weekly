Alaska is about to commit $272M through the Rural Health Transformation Program to AI-enabled telehealth. The program is barred from funding the one thing that telehealth needs to run.

Governor Mike Dunleavy framed RHTP as a turning point. “This could be the beginning,” he told the Anchorage Daily News in January, “the moment that not only Alaska helped transform the United States, but we helped transform the world when it comes to rural health, and the advancement in AI and data is going to be phenomenal.” He isn’t wrong about the ambition. RHTP is the largest rural-health innovation fund the state has ever received.

The optimism is reasonable on its face. RHTP’s rules deliberately exclude new facilities and Medicaid service replacement, which channels the money toward technology. Remote patient monitoring, drone delivery, and AI-assisted diagnostics are mature enough to stretch the reach of Alaska’s thin specialist workforce across 165 plus villages. If even a fraction of the 403 projects now advancing to full application deploy well, the gains could be real and exportable as a national model.

Here is where the frame breaks. The Alaska Department of Health’s own RHTP rules state categorically that the funds cannot pay for broadband infrastructure, construction-based installation, or monthly internet service. The program funds the application but not the pipe that application depends on. And the pipe is thin. The HRSA-backed Telehealth Broadband Project evaluated 42 Alaska healthcare locations and found only 6.6% met the 100/20 Mbps benchmark that telehealth requires, with an average median download speed of 27.1 Mbps, less than a third of standard. State Associate Director for Health Betsy Wood said it plainly, that “we’re all a little disappointed to see that we can’t use this funding to build some of those hard infrastructure pieces that we do sorely need across the state.”

So the more accurate frame looks like this.

- RHTP is a genuine win for technology-enabled care, and AI telehealth is a real beneficiary.
- The binding constraint is connectivity, not algorithms.
- 93.4% of evaluated healthcare sites fail the speed standard the tools assume.
- Connectivity has to be solved in parallel through BEAD, ReConnect, or other vehicles.

The application window closes in late June. Applicants and the Department of Health are deciding right now whether to fund AI deployments in locations where they may not reliably run, which puts $272M at stranded-asset risk if the connectivity precondition stays unaddressed.

Should RHTP applicants be required to show a funded broadband path before an AI telehealth award clears, or does that gate the very villages the money is meant to reach?
