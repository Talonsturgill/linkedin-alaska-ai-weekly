The Air Force’s AI data center lease bids close May 29 across 4,700 acres at JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS. Chugach Electric and Golden Valley Electric have already told the Air Force they can’t supply the gas to power them.

That’s the binding constraint nobody priced in. Under the DAF Enhanced Use Lease model, winning bidders self-fund power and water procurement, so the utilities’ position isn’t advisory. It’s a hard ceiling on what any bidder can credibly propose by the 29th. Chugach’s Hilcorp Alaska gas contract expires in 2028. Cook Inlet supply is dwindling on the curve ADN laid out May 5. The fuel curve and the federal AI buildout curve are pointed in opposite directions, and the federal one has the deadline.

The conventional read of the JBER push is that this is a chips-and-models story. That framing missed the wall. The decisive variables this week aren’t models or compute. They’re gas molecules, statutory clocks in Juneau, and a labor pool that’s contracting while the workload arrives.

Juneau has two statutes on a May 20 session-end clock. HB 217 requires a credentialed human safety operator in every commercial AV at 10,000 lb or under. Teamsters Local 959 backs it, AV industry trade groups oppose. SB 111, the right-to-repair bill, passed the Senate 15 to 5 on May 11 and forces Apple, Amazon, Google, and peers to supply parts and documentation to independent shops at parity with authorized providers. Two statute-level decisions compressed into eight days, in a session where the AI data center bids close nine days after gavel-out.

The labor side is worse than the headlines suggest. Per Alaska Business Magazine on May 8, citing DOLWD March data, the Information sector sits at 4,100 jobs, down 4.7%. Federal civilian employment is at 13,300, down 12.5%, a loss of 1,900 positions. The workforce that an AI buildout at JBER and Eielson would draw against is shrinking in exactly the categories that matter.

One door did open. UAF signed a three-year MOU with the Defense Innovation Unit on May 8, announced by Sen. Sullivan, with initial focus on energy systems through ACEP and drones through ACUASI. No capital attached. It’s a permission slip, not a check. But ACEP is exactly the shop that should be modeling what a 2028 Hilcorp cliff means for a data center load.

Federal AI dollars are arriving on a procurement clock. Alaska’s gas supply, statute book, and labor bench are running slower clocks the wrong direction. Both decisions land before fiscal year end, and both shape the AI economy the state gets to build.

If the utilities can’t supply the gas and Juneau adjourns without a clear AV and repair posture, what does a serious May 29 bid actually look like?
