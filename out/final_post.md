The Air Force posted its Enhanced Use Lease solicitation on June 5, offering up to 4,700 acres at JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS to private AI data center developers. Proposals close June 29. For the first time, commercial AI compute could occupy federal military land in Alaska.

The DAF Enhanced Use Lease mechanism under 10 USC 2667 converts non-excess military real property into private AI data center campuses, with the developer bearing all capital risk and the Air Force receiving in-kind consideration.

Five layers control this mechanism.

- [WHITE HOUSE EO AUTHORITY]. EOs 14179, 14156, and 14154 direct the Secretary of Defense to identify federal land for AI data center construction. Office of the President, implemented by SAF/IE.
- [SAF/IE PROGRAM APPROVAL]. Robert Moriarty, Deputy Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Installations, authorized the RFLP and the 4,700-acre parcel portfolio. SAF/IE.
- [AFCEC/CI SOLICITATION GATE]. Air Force Civil Engineer Center issues, evaluates, and awards or rejects proposals under 10 USC 2667. AFCEC/CI Installations Directorate.
- [UTILITY INTERCONNECT AND GAS SUPPLY]. Chugach Electric's Hilcorp gas contract expires in 2028. Cook Inlet gas has dropped to less than 1% of GVEA's fuel supply. No developer executes without a utility load-service commitment. Chugach Electric Association (JBER), GVEA (Eielson, Clear SFS).
- [PRIVATE DEVELOPER CAPITAL COMMITMENT]. Developer finances, permits, builds, and operates at full private risk. RFLP respondent, due June 29.

The chokepoint is AFCEC/CI's accept-or-reject decision on the June 29 proposals. That binary is final for this cycle, with no federal appeal path for developers if AFCEC issues a no-award determination. Every other layer flows through that single gate.

The utility layer, not the military layer, is the real constraint. The EUL structure resists budget pressure because the Air Force spends nothing and holds title throughout. But Chugach Electric's 2028 Hilcorp gas contract creates a hard window. A developer who wins an EUL at JBER has roughly 24 months to build before that contract lapses or lock in an alternative supply. That's a forcing function for Cook Inlet gas and LNG investment that reaches well past this RFLP.

Any developer evaluating an Alaska military EUL bid must, before June 29, secure a written indication of interest from Chugach or GVEA on load-service feasibility that includes post-2028 gas assumptions. A proposal without that path fails at the utility layer even after AFCEC awards it. If AFCEC awards by Q3 2026, Alaska lands the first commercial AI compute footprint on federal military land in the Arctic. If utilities don't commit, this window closes for another cycle.

Should AFCEC treat a utility indication-of-interest as a mandatory proposal element, or does requiring it risk excluding developers best positioned to solve the power problem independently?
