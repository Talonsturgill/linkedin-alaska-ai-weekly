This week a Native regional corporation put AI to work in front of more than 15,000 shareholders, the same week Washington left 91 Alaska tribes holding roughly $54 million in frozen energy grants and posted another year of federal job losses in the state. Two clocks are running in opposite directions, and Native institutions are absorbing both.

Start with the contraction, because it’s the real story. Alaska total nonfarm employment was 332,300 in April, essentially flat year over year. Underneath that flat line, federal civilian employment fell 1,900 jobs, down 12.5%. The information sector, the bucket where an AI economy would actually show up, sits at 4,100 and is still shrinking, per Alaska DOLWD. The grid money tells the same story from the capital side. As of late May, Alaska tribes had drawn less than 6% of the $58 million promised under the IIJA Grid Resilience formula program, with roughly $54 million frozen after federal spending-review orders, according to Alaska Public Media and KNOM. White Mountain, about 250 people on the Seward Peninsula, fronted roughly $700,000 on generators and cooling upgrades expecting reimbursement, and has received nothing.

Against that, NANA Regional Corporation shipped something real. On May 26 NANA and Geographic Solutions launched NANA Works, an employment platform built on VOS Sapphire AI, putting resume tools, interview prep, and job recommendations in front of its 15,000-plus Iñupiat shareholders. It’s the first Alaska Native regional corporation to field a purpose-built AI workforce platform at scale. Worth noting who moved first here. Not the state, not a Lower 48 prime, but a Native corporation building its own onramp while the federal one closes.

The forward risk runs through the same institutions. At a May 22 Senate Indian Affairs hearing she chairs, Sen. Lisa Murkowski flagged an FY2027 request that, as reported by Tribal Business News, proposes a $743.7 million cut to the Bureau of Indian Affairs and a $436.9 million cut to the Bureau of Indian Education, plus a $93 million cut to IHS sanitation confirmed on the committee’s own page. Those cuts hit the 638 self-determination contracts that ANC subsidiaries and tribal health organizations run on.

And the replacement economy is still at the bid stage. On May 28 the Air Force pushed its Alaska AI data center lease deadline on solicitation AFCEC-26-R-0006 from May 29 to June 29. The federal jobs are leaving on a fixed clock. The AI buildout meant to backfill them keeps slipping its own.

When the federal scaffolding contracts faster than the AI economy can stand up, who underwrites the gap year, the state, the ANCs, or no one?
