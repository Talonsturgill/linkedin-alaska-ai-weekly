Sparrow Mahoney, founder and CEO of STAK Energy, just moved a $500M off-grid AI data center one procedural step closer to a 50-year North Slope lease. On May 12 the Alaska DNR issued a preliminary best-interest determination on 715.4 acres.

Mahoney runs STAK Energy out of Anchorage, where the company is domiciled, even though the proposed project site sits on the Arctic North Slope. The bio is short on purpose. The decision is the post.

Here’s what actually happened, per ADN (adn.com/business-economy/2026/05/18/a-huge-data-center-could-rise-on-alaskas-north-slope) and Northern Journal (northernjournal.com/a-huge-data-center-could-rise-on-alaskas-north-slope). STAK filed the lease application with DNR in November 2025. DNR solicited competing bids and received none. On May 12, 2026, DNR issued a preliminary determination that leasing the 715.4 acres to STAK is in the state’s best interest. The public comment period runs through June 15. STAK has proposed a campus that could ultimately draw up to three gigawatts of power for its own use, generated by an on-site natural gas plant, with a peak construction workforce around 1,500 and roughly 60 permanent jobs. The binary Mahoney owned was site model and lease track, off-grid Arctic gas on state land versus a grid-tied or alternative-site path. He chose off-grid.

The desk reads this as mediocre-to-speculative. The strategic logic is real. Stranded North Slope gas plus Arctic free cooling is a genuine Alaska comparative advantage, and that argument deserves a hearing. But a DNR preliminary best-interest determination is a procedural step any unopposed application can achieve, and the project around it has structural gaps. No disclosed outside equity financing. No named anchor tenant or offtake agreement. No interconnection, by design, because the campus would be entirely off-grid at gigawatt scale, which is itself an unproven model. The gas supply story leans on stranded North Slope volumes, but the gathering and processing infrastructure to deliver them to this site has not been announced. The same site was previously pitched by Mahoney for crypto mining, and STAK officials declined to answer specific questions from reporters at both ADN and Northern Journal. That’s a lot of weight on one procedural milestone.

The next binary is the one that matters. Can STAK respond substantively to public comments, move DNR toward a final lease, and disclose a lead equity investor, an anchor tenant, or a gas supply agreement before the comment period closes June 15. If financing isn’t named by then, the credibility on the 3 GW figure deteriorates fast. Watch the docket and the press releases, in that order.

What would actually move this from speculative to bankable, a named anchor tenant, a disclosed equity lead, or a firm gas gathering agreement?
