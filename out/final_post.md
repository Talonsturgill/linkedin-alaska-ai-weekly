The Department of Energy named an Anchorage firm to an $89 million grant on June 4 to study a 1,250-megawatt coal plant with carbon capture in the Susitna watershed. Latitude Media reports the DOE-funded plants are built to power data centers. The money is chasing the wrong layer.

The conventional read is clean. Power is the bottleneck on Alaska AI, so federal capital for new baseload is the unlock. Terra Energy Center Corp., an affiliate of Canadian firm Flatlands Energy, would market the load near Port MacKenzie, and Chugach Electric and Golden Valley Electric weigh whether to anchor to it. The $89M figure is from ADN.

That framing answers a bottleneck the near-term money doesn’t have. The plant has no permitted mine, no finalized design, and no signed power-purchase agreement. It can’t deliver an electron until the 2030s. Meanwhile the AI dollars that actually pencil in Alaska this year need no new generation at all. The binding constraints on the work shipping now are clearances, data, compliance, and people.

Look at what’s already moving on its own clock.

- NSF reopened SBIR/STTR project pitches June 2 toward a July 27 deadline, deploying $250 million nationally, with Phase I awards reportedly up to $305,000 and Alaska applicants supported by the UAF FAST program and Alaska SBDC.
- HB 117 sits on Gov. Dunleavy’s desk as of June 1 with a June 18 clock, letting Fish and Game draw on the NOAA-approved computer-vision catch footage trawl vessels already run.
- The $272 million Rural Health Transformation portal opened June 1 with a June 22 deadline, with AI clinical tools and drone delivery as fundable categories and 403 organizations advanced.

None of those needs a watt of new power. Each has a deadline inside the next eight weeks.

The lock-in cuts two ways. The coal study can’t produce capacity before the decade turns, so $89M of federal attention lands on a plant that anchors nothing this cycle while the deployable money sets its winners now. And the rulebook is moving outside Alaska too. The June 4 bipartisan House discussion draft from Reps. Obernolte and Trahan would preempt state AI laws for three years. Compare the coal bet to the Air Force AI data-center lease for JBER, Eielson, and Clear, amended May 28 to a June 29 deadline, where developers carry all financing, permitting, and construction cost. Private capital builds the power-hungry layer on its own dime. Public capital is funding the speculative one.

When federal AI dollars reach Alaska, should they fund baseload that pencils in the 2030s or the deployable layers with deadlines this summer, and who decides?
