On June 1 Golden Valley Electric Association raised its quarterly fuel charge 61 percent, from 12.78 cents to 20.65 cents per kWh, the largest hike in the co-op’s history. GVEA powers Fairbanks and Eielson Air Force Base, one of three Alaska bases the Air Force just offered up for AI data centers.

The pitch sells itself. Alaska has stranded gas, cold air for free cooling, and federal land at JBER, Clear Space Force Station, and Eielson, where lease proposals closed May 29. The story Washington and the trade press keep telling is that cheap northern energy makes the state the next hosting frontier for GPU compute.

That framing prices the wrong number. The binding constraint on a data center was never the wellhead, it’s the delivered grid price at the meter. GVEA CEO Travis Million tied the increase to diesel roughly doubling toward $5.10 a gallon after Strait of Hormuz disruptions, record April demand, and lost Cook Inlet gas imports. At 20.65 cents per kWh in fuel charge alone, before everything else on the bill, grid-connected hyperscale at Eielson doesn’t pencil. No source tied the hike to the base directly, and the desk isn’t claiming one did. But the math is the math. A megawatt-hungry tenant either builds behind-the-meter generation or walks.

So look at the layer that already pencils this week. On May 28 Koniag Emerging Technologies, an Anchorage-headquartered Alaska Native Corporation subsidiary, won a DOD firm-fixed-price contract worth up to $24.4 million through 2030, supporting Washington Headquarters Services financial systems and the ADVANA analytics platform. That award needs zero megawatts. It’s people, clearances, and past performance.

Then read what the federal government actually shipped north on June 2. The new executive order on advanced AI directs CISA, within 30 days, to extend AI-enabled cybersecurity tools to rural hospitals, community banks, and local utilities. Alaska has among the most Critical Access Hospitals per capita and a fleet of thinly-staffed rural co-ops, the exact profile the order names. CISA Region 10 covers the state, and binding guidance lands by early July.

Stack those three facts and the frontier pitch inverts.

- Hosting layer, megawatt-bound, dead at 20.65 cents until the meter problem is solved.
- Services and analytics layer, where Koniag already books real DOD dollars.
- Policy and tooling layer, where the CISA order arrives free for the entities that claim it.

What Washington sends Alaska is cyber tools and policy, not cheaper electrons. The capital that chases hosting now is chasing a number GVEA just moved the wrong way.

Which entity should claim the CISA tooling first, the rural health consortia or the electric co-ops staring at that fuel charge?
