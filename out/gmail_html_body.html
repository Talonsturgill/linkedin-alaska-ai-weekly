The Alaska DNR is deciding whether Stak Energy gets a 50-year lease on North Slope state land for an AI data center and power plant. More than 500 public comments landed before the deadline. The comments aren't the gate.

The state-land disposal process that decides Stak's tenure runs through the Department of Natural Resources in four stages.

- Lease-application intake and administration of the 50-year North Slope lease, run by the DNR Division of Mining, Land and Water under AS 38.05.070/.073/.075.
- Written best-interest finding plus public notice and comment (minimum 30 days, extended to July 17, 2026 after the 500-plus comments), made by the DMLW Director under AS 38.05.035(e).
- Final Finding and Decision requiring Commissioner consent, held by the Commissioner of Natural Resources under AS 38.05.035(e).
- Post-decision 20-day appeal window plus 10-day Commissioner reconsideration, controlled by the DNR Commissioner.

The leverage sits in stage three. AS 38.05.035(e) lets the DMLW Director approve the lease only with the consent of the Commissioner, and hands the Commissioner final authority for transactions above the rental threshold. A 50-year industrial lease clears that threshold easily. So one statutory officer, the Commissioner of the Alaska Department of Natural Resources, owns a single approve or deny gate over the whole disposal. Not a board, not a committee. One signature.

That reframes what the 500-plus comments actually are. As the Copper River Country Journal reported on July 6, “More than 500 public comments were received before a preliminary deadline set by the Alaska Department of Natural Resources.” Those comments are input to the best-interest finding, not the decision itself. The Commissioner can weigh them and consent anyway. The revolving-door context is real and worth naming, Stak's chief strategy officer John Boyle was DNR commissioner from 2023 to 2025 and is now seeking a lease from his former agency, but it doesn't change the machinery. The same consent gate exists no matter who holds either chair.

So the binding date isn't the comment volume, it's what the Commissioner does after the July 17 comment close. Anyone with exposure to North Slope siting, Interior or Slope power, or state-land AI precedent should watch the Final Finding and Decision, then the 20-day appeal and 10-day reconsideration window. That's the only stretch where the disposal can still be reopened.

When one best-interest finding decides a 50-year, precedent-setting lease, is public comment a real check on the Commissioner's consent, or just the record it gets weighed against?
