# Cold Take selection — NO. 07 — 2026-06-03

## The claim
Governor Mike Dunleavy: **"you might be looking at four or five cents a kilowatt hour for decades and decades to come"** (Data Center Knowledge, 2025-10-07; repeated at the Data Center World keynote, Broadband Breakfast, 2026-04-24).

## Steelman
Alaska holds one of the largest untapped gas reserves on U.S. soil. If Alaska LNG reaches financial close and is built, the capital amortizes over a very long project life, and delivered power to an anchor industrial customer could land in the 4-6 cent range. With Lower-48 developers signing PPAs at 7-12 cents and waiting years in grid queues, even a credible prospect of nickel power for 20-plus years is a rational reason to look north.

## Corrective thesis (one sentence)
The 4-5 cent figure is a best-case price from an unbuilt, not-yet-financed pipeline that the state's own legislative leadership and largest utilities call contested, not a power cost a data center operator can underwrite today.

## Evidence spine
- GaffneyCline analyst Nick Fulford (via Rep. Calvin Schrage), House Finance, 2026-05-26: Alaska LNG has "a very small window of break-even profitability"; costs may reach $57-60B. (Newsminer wire, 2026-05-29)
- Senate Majority Leader Cathy Giessel: developer timeline "completely unrealistic"; no financial-fitness certification yet. (ADN, 2026-02-11)
- Chugach Electric (Julie Hasquet): "We currently do not have gas to serve a large data center." Hilcorp contract ends 2028; Cook Inlet share of GVEA fuel fell from ~30% to under 1%. (ADN, 2026-05-05)
- Global gas-turbine backlog "as much as seven years." (Alaska Public Media, 2026-05-26)

## AK business consequence
The 4-5 cent price anchors the state's active pitch to hyperscalers weighing 100-500 MW commitments through 2026-27. If site selection proceeds on that assumption and AKLNG slips or fails, the siting decisions, state lease and tax revenue, and the AI build-out evaporate before ground is broken.
