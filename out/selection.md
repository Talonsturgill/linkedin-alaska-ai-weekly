# Received Wisdom NO. 28 — Selection — 2026-07-02

## Selected claim
"The AI data center load shape is changing and data architectures have advanced, so places like Alaska's North Slope become not only viable, but we argue that they have become inevitable."
— Sparrow Mahoney, CEO, STAK Energy, at the March 2026 DOE/ORNL workshop on AI data-center power and security (ORNL proceedings, 2026-03-16). Recirculated via ADN (2026-05-18) and multiple Alaska outlets in May 2026.

## Steelman
North Slope AI data centers bypass the Railbelt grid constraints actively preventing Southcentral Alaska from hosting large industrial loads. Former DNR analyst Antony Scott himself said North Slope operators would "likely be willing to sell gas" and a remote location avoids driving up urban electricity prices. Modern AI inference workloads tolerate latency better than earlier architectures. Available land, natural cold, proximity to one of North America's largest gas accumulations, and zero grid competition make a genuinely distinctive industrial proposition, conditional on gas contracts and a pipeline spur.

## Corrective thesis (one sentence)
The North Slope AI data center is conditionally possible, not inevitable: as of mid-2026 STAK had no firm gas supply agreement, its DNR filing leaves the pipeline route at 25 to 90 miles with no source field named, and the project would need more than twice the gas urban Alaska uses in total, through gas-to-market infrastructure that mostly does not exist.

## Evidence spine
1. ADN 2026-05-18: no firm gas supply agreement; pipeline route "anywhere between 25 and 90 miles"; Antony Scott: "That means they don't have a gas supply."
2. ADN 2026-05-18: plant would consume "more than twice as much" gas as urban Alaska's total electrical generation + heating.
3. ADN 2026-05-18: North Slope operators have "almost exclusively extracted oil"; "minimal infrastructure exists to move North Slope natural gas to market."
4. ADN 2026-05-05: Chugach (Hasquet): "We currently do not have gas to serve a large data center"; Pickett: Southcentral electric industry "in a world of hurt"; Hilcorp contract concludes 2028 with no replacement.
5. ADN 2026-05-05: GVEA Cook Inlet gas share fell ~30% → <1% in a year; near-emergency LNG imports.
6. ORNL 2026-03-16 (primary): Mahoney positioned the project as "powered by natural gas from the adjacent Prudhoe Bay field," which the DNR application's undetermined 25-90 mile route contradicts.

## AK consequence
Alaska DNR land decision on a one-square-mile North Slope site; capital partners sizing a $500M, 3 GW commitment; state officials and gubernatorial candidates citing "inevitable" as validation of Alaska's AI industrial strategy. Committing capital or land before gas contracts exist is a bet on an undemonstrated supply assumption.

## Why this over the alternatives
Only candidate to clear all six gates. All fresh-territory candidates (PFD AI, RHTP healthcare AI, Arctic satellite claims) failed gate 5 (counter-evidence sources unreachable: 403/404/503). Distinct correction mechanism from all six prior data-center corrections (gas supply contracting, not power price/cooling/jobs/fiber/land-lease readiness). Seventh consecutive data-center-cluster correction, noted for the Editor's note.
