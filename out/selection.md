# Selection — Received Wisdom NO. 10 (2026-06-06)

## The claim (verbatim)
> "Since satellites don't work up here, we've got to figure out how to use all the other sensors, right?"
> — Troy Boufard, Director, UAF Center for Arctic Security and Resilience; Research Fellow, U.S. Military Academy at West Point. MeriTalk, 20 Jan 2026.

Independently echoed by Brig. Gen. Houston Cantwell (Ret.), AFA Mitchell Institute, in Air and Space Forces Magazine (14 Nov 2025), under the broader "space-based assets fail at northern latitudes" framing.

## Steelman (state it at its strongest, fairly)
Geostationary satellites genuinely cannot illuminate polar latitudes above roughly 70-75 degrees North. That is a hard physical limitation, not a procurement excuse. And even where low-Earth-orbit constellations carry data, space-based moving-target radar (the surveillance function, not connectivity) is still immature for near-real-time Arctic tracking. The sensor-fusion problem Boufard is pointing at is real and urgent. Smart Arctic-ops people who learned the trade in the pre-LEO era carry "satellites don't work up here" as settled fact, and for the GEO belt they are right.

## One-sentence corrective thesis
The load-bearing distinction for Arctic AI sensor investment is not "satellites versus everything else," it's GEO-belt satellites versus LEO polar constellations, and the LEO half already works over Alaska today, so an unqualified "satellites don't work up here" quietly tilts billions in domain-awareness capex toward terrestrial-only architectures on a faulty premise.

## Evidence spine (all primary or re-verified)
- Iridium NEXT: 66 satellites, 86.4-degree near-polar inclination, 6 planes, 24/7 polar coverage including the GMDSS Sea Area A4 maritime-safety zone that GEO satellites cannot reach. Operational at 82 degrees North (Arctic Lynx, June 2021). [ESA eoPortal, primary]
- Starlink: polar-orbit shell launched 2022, service confirmed active in Alaska and the Canadian Arctic; ~40,000-satellite constellation planned. [High North News]
- The MeriTalk piece itself lists "polar-orbiting satellites" among Boufard's proposed alternative sensors, so the GEO/LEO distinction is genuinely not being drawn even by the expert making the claim.

## Alaska business consequence
Arctic Domain Awareness procurement (NORAD/USNORTHCOM 2026-2030 modernization, potential DAF Alaska sensor solicitations). If program offices and the Alaska-based contractors competing for those contracts accept "satellites don't work" unqualified, LEO-integrated architectures that already function over Alaska get underweighted, and terrestrial-only pitches win priority on a premise that is two-thirds wrong. The corrected frame tells an allocator exactly which line item to re-examine: any Arctic sensor business case whose justification rests on a blanket satellite-coverage gap.
