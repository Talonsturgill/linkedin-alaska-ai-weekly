# VOL. 04 Selection — Alaska.Ai LinkedIn — 2026-06-03

## Mode
**Deep Dive.** One structural tension dominates the window: Alaska is deploying AI and underwriting AI-scale capital decisions faster than it is governing AI at all. The 34th Legislature adjourned May 20 having killed every standalone AI bill it had, leaving the state with no AI statute, while the same window shows real AI shipping (wildfire drones), real AI money opening (a $272M health grant with a June 22 clock), and a capital disclosure that resets the AI-power premise (Glenfarne LNG). That is a genuine false-binary to take a position on, so Deep Dive over Weekly Brief.

## Package angle
Alaska's legislature just proved it has no intention of governing AI any time soon. Every standalone AI bill, autonomous-vehicle regulation (HB 217), state-agency AI and deepfake rules (SB 2), the AI policy bill (HB 47), and even the study task force (HCR 3), died at the May 20 adjournment. The lone survivor, HB 239's criminal penalty for AI-generated CSAM, sits on Dunleavy's desk with a June 18 clock. The conventional read is that a regulatory vacuum is a light-touch, pro-business win. That read is wrong about who fills the vacuum. AI is already operating in the state with no state framework: a Division of Forestry infrared-drone module just contained a fire at 14.8 acres near Delta Junction, and the state's $272M Rural Health Transformation grant opened a window June 1 (closing June 22) that will fund AI clinical tools and drone pharmacy delivery in rural communities. Meanwhile Glenfarne's June 3 disclosure that the Alaska LNG pipeline will cost $44.5B to $54.5B reset the power math under every gigawatt-class AI data-center pitch in the state. A vacuum doesn't stay empty. It gets filled by federal terms (the June 2 White House AI EO and CISA's July 2 clock) and by procurement rulebooks Juneau didn't write. The frame for the LinkedIn audience: Alaska is becoming an AI deployment and capital story with no Alaska AI rulebook behind it, and the people writing the rules now sit in Washington and in federal grant offices, not in Juneau.

Lead frame is NEW. It does not re-lead the last four weeks: data-center grid economics (06-02), state-vs-federal authority/HB239 enactment (06-01), autonomy-vs-substrate (05-31), or federal-contraction/Native-institutions (05-29). HB 239 appears here only as the lone-survivor footnote, not the lead.
