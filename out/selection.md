## Package angle
The federal AI buildout finally cut Alaska a check this week, and the structure of that check is the story. A federal contract record reported June 5 shows Koniag Advisory and Business Solutions, a subsidiary of the Kodiak-region Alaska Native Corporation Koniag, winning a five-year $18M DOE contract through 8(a) sole source to support the agency's SBIR/STTR programs. That is the value Alaska captured, and it is narrow on two axes. First, it came through ANC 8(a) preference, the one procurement lane Alaska reliably wins, rather than competitive past-performance in the broader commercial base post_001 worried about. Second, it bought program support, the grant back office, not the AI workloads themselves. The same week shows the other capture channels are equally narrow and time-boxed. NSF's TechAccess AI-Ready America ($168M to $224M nationally, $1M a year for three years per state hub) closes its Round 1 letter of intent June 16, and Alaska has to decide whether the University of Alaska System files or sits out until December while ten states build first. And the rules that will govern all of it are being written in Washington: the Great American AI Act discussion draft (June 4) would preempt state AI law for three years, which is why AFA Council 30, the Anchorage flight attendants at Alaska Airlines, went public June 1 against it. The frame for the LinkedIn audience: Alaska's share of the federal AI economy is flowing through a handful of preference lanes and grant deadlines, each contingent and each closing, while the terms get set Outside.
