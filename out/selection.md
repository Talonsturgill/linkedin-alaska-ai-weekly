# Received Wisdom — Selection — NO. 17 — 2026-06-15

## The claim (as it circulates)
> "Since satellites don't work up here, we've got to figure out how to use all the other sensors, right?"
> — Troy Boufard, Director, Center for Arctic Security and Resilience, University of Alaska Fairbanks (MeriTalk, 20 Jan 2026)

Echoed across the Arctic-defense analyst community: FPRI (Boulègue, Pincus, Lipson, Nov 2025) and CSIS (Kari Bingen, Jan 2026). Circulation: 4 independently originated sources.

## Steelman (the strongest honest version)
Geosynchronous communications and ISR satellites genuinely cannot deliver reliable, persistent coverage above roughly 65 to 70 degrees North. Low elevation angles and atmospheric interference are real and well documented. The gap drove the Space Force's Arctic Satellite Broadband Mission and the US-Norway LEO discussions. A planner working from a GEO baseline has good reason to lean on ground, maritime, and airborne sensors. Building AI sensor-fusion around non-satellite inputs is a rational hedge.

## Corrective thesis (one sentence)
"Satellites don't work up here" was true of geosynchronous birds and is now false of low-earth orbit, and treating it as a categorical fact pushes Alaska defense AI money toward ground-sensor build-out and away from the LEO/SAR fusion layer that already exists and pays the highest marginal return.

## Evidence spine (primary first)
1. **Breaking Defense, 23 Feb 2026 (primary).** The Army's 11th Airborne used Starshield (military Starlink) terminals at JPMRC Rotation 26-02 in the Yukon Training Area near Fairbanks. The only reported limit was line-of-sight through camouflage netting, a concealment trade-off, not absent coverage. The link worked.
2. **FPRI, 12 Nov 2025 (same analyst community).** SAR imaging already gives "year-round, near-real time imagery" in the Arctic and an Arctic-dedicated LEO constellation is "a welcome addition." The community circulating the gap narrative concedes functional coverage.

## AK business stakes
DoD program officers and Alaska-based defense contractors (Fort Greely Next Generation Interceptor sustainment, the Golden Dome Alaska intercept layer) are scoping AI sensing investment for FY26 to FY27. A "satellites don't work" baseline biases those decisions toward ground-only fusion and away from the LEO/SAR integration layer, which is exactly where Alaska's highest-value defense-tech contract exposure sits.
