## Mechanism
The Anchorage utility capacity statement: AO 2026-27 / AMC Title 21 data center conditional-use gate.

Anchorage made data centers (defined at 20 MW or more of peak electrical demand) a conditional use in
industrial districts only, and made a written system-capacity statement from the serving electrical
utility a mandatory component of the permit application. That delegates the binding siting
determination to a non-municipal, member-owned cooperative sitting upstream of any public hearing.

## Chokepoint
L3. The serving electrical utility (Chugach Electric Association in the Anchorage Bowl) issues or
withholds a written statement of sufficient system capacity at peak demand. It is a mandatory
application component, not a criterion weighed in the balance: no statement, no complete application,
no hearing. No municipal override, no published timeline, and, unlike the wastewater presumption, no
rebuttal path written into the code.

## Structural read
Anchorage built the gate before the applicants arrived, and converted a private interconnection study
into a precondition for a public land-use approval. The visible layer is the five-vote P&Z hearing,
which is a committee and not where leverage lives. The leverage is a load study run by a cooperative
that answers to its ratepayers, not the Assembly, on no clock. The ordinance also writes its own
escape: every utility-statement requirement is conditioned on connecting to the grid, public water, or
public sewer. Behind-the-meter generation with closed-loop water triggers none of them. Anchorage has
priced grid-connected compute and left off-grid compute comparatively unpriced.

## Forward implication
Treat the utility capacity statement, not the zoning hearing, as the schedule-driving milestone. The
copying window is open now: Mat-Su has no data-center rule at all and FNSB's comprehensive plan is in
draft, with the 2026-08-27 AML conference positioning Anchorage as the statewide template. The
question to put to both: does a withheld electrical statement get any rebuttal path, given Anchorage
gave one to wastewater and none to power.
