# VOL. 01 Selection — 2026-05-13

## Mode
**Deep Dive.** S. 98's May 11 signing is the single sharp in-window event with a clean structural angle (formulaic subsidy → discretionary vetting) and a hard date (Dec 31 Alaska Plan sunset). Three supporting stories ladder up into the same argument: every actor with skin in Alaska's connectivity-and-federal-services game just got handed a new rulebook in the same week.

## Package angle
The federal funding architecture under rural Alaska connectivity changed on May 11. The Rural Broadband Protection Act (S. 98), signed into law by the President, replaces the formulaic Alaska Plan eligibility model with an FCC-administered vetting process — and the Alaska Plan dies December 31. The post-Plan successor program, the Alaska Connect Fund (live through 2034), is captured by the new vetting too. While Juneau argues over data center ratepayer law and Dunleavy decides whether to sign HB 78, Alaska's dominant carrier just committed $290M of 2026 capex against an eligibility framework whose rules are still being written. Koniag's $147.5M USDA win this week shows the federal services pipeline still flows to AK actors who navigate the architecture. The frame: the rules under Alaska's federal connectivity revenue stack just changed in plain sight, and the response window is six months.

## Lineup

### LEAD — D3: Rural Broadband Protection Act (S. 98) signed May 11; Alaska Plan expires December 31
- President signed S. 98 on May 11, 2026, per White House Office of the Press Secretary briefing.
- New law requires FCC rulemaking establishing financial and operational vetting for high-cost universal service broadband applicants and recipients.
- Alaska Plan (USAC) provides 10-year fixed-formula support to carriers serving high-cost Alaska; expires Dec 31, 2026.
- Successor Alaska Connect Fund (live 2025-2034) is also captured by the new vetting framework.
- Alaska is more per-capita high-cost USF dependent than any other state.
- Confidence: high. Primary sources: White House, GovInfo (S. 98 enrolled), USAC.
- DO NOT use the "FCC rulemaking by Nov 8" / 180-day deadline — validator confirmed that specific date appears in no verified source. Stick to "rulemaking now triggered" / "rulemaking on a clock the FCC has yet to publish."
