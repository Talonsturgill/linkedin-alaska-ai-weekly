# The Stack — Selection, 21 August 2026

**CATEGORY: REGULATORY** (kicker line renders `THE STACK · REGULATORY · 21 AUG 2026`)

## Mechanism
FERC preliminary permit site-banking under FPA sec. 4(f) / 16 U.S.C. 798, and the
18 CFR 4.32(e) deficiency-and-acceptance gate that manufactures the priority date.

A preliminary permit authorizes no construction. Its only legal effect is to freeze a
first-in-time priority to file a hydropower license application over a defined water
corridor for up to four years, extendable four more. Whether the applicant keeps its
original filing date, and therefore its priority, is decided by a paperwork-conformance
determination inside FERC's Office of Energy Projects.

## News trigger
Anchorage Daily News (Bella Biondini), 19 August 2026. Delaware-based DeepGreen Cook
Inlet SPV LLC proposed a 100 MW underwater AI data center off Nikiski, powered and
cooled by Cook Inlet tides, with as many 350 underwater turbines and 66 "hives" housing
AI servers. FERC's Northwest Branch issued a deficiency letter on 18 June 2026.
ADN never uses the phrase "preliminary permit" and never explains what the filing does.

## Chokepoint
Layer 2, the 18 CFR 4.32(e) deficiency-and-acceptance determination.
**Actor:** Director, FERC Office of Energy Projects.
**Binary decision:** determine the cured application conforms (preserving the original
filing date as the site-priority date and triggering the Secretary's notice), or
determine it does not (any resubmission carries a new filing date and the Nikiski
corridor priority claim collapses to the back of the queue).
**Why asymmetric:** the permit's entire value is priority in time. That asset is
manufactured by a paperwork call vested in one office, made before any commissioner
votes, before any environmental review, and before any Alaska party can legally file a
competing application, because the competing window does not open until this office says
the application is complete. Not a committee, not a hearing. A letter.

## Structural read
Site-banking, not power development. A Delaware SPV formed in January 2026, one month
before filing, is using a 1920 hydropower statute to place a four-to-eight-year exclusive
priority claim over Alaska seabed with an instrument that costs almost nothing and
requires no environmental review to obtain. The AI framing is not decoration, a 100 MW
behind-the-meter compute buyer solves the offtake problem that killed every prior Cook
Inlet tidal proposal. The lesson is about standing and timing. The parties with the most
to lose (AEA, Homer Electric, the borough, the tribes) are structurally last to know and
legally last to act.

## Forward implication
AEA, Homer Electric Association and the Kenai Peninsula Borough need a motion to
intervene, and if warranted a competing preliminary permit application, drafted and ready
BEFORE FERC issues the acceptance notice. The clock runs from notice issuance, not from
discovery, and FERC does not extend it for parties who were not monitoring. Set an
eLibrary alert on Cook Inlet hydrokinetic filings now.
