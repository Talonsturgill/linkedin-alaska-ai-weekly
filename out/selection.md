## Package angle
Autonomy stopped being a proposal in Alaska this week. A V-BAT vertical-takeoff drone flew live ISR off the Coast Guard cutter Waesche through a 113-day Bering Sea patrol, the first operational integration of the system on a national security cutter in Alaskan waters. That is hardware in the water, not a slide deck. But the same week exposed how thin the rails under it are. A single subsea fiber cut knocked six Aleutian communities offline for up to two weeks, the second such break in roughly six months. And an NSF AI-workforce program set a June 16 letter-of-intent deadline that Alaska's institutions can either meet or miss. The frame for capital allocators and program managers: federal demand for autonomous and AI capability anchored in Alaska is now real and recurring, but the connectivity, workforce, and contracting rails that decide whether Alaska captures the work or just hosts it for outside firms are each running on a different clock. Koniag's Tuknik unit just proved an Alaska entity can win national recognition for federal AI. The open question is whether that is the exception or the onramp.
