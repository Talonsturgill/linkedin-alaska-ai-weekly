# Cold Take — Issue NO. 02 — Selection

## The claim (verbatim)

> "you might be looking at four or five cents a kilowatt hour for decades and decades to come"

— Governor Mike Dunleavy, speaking at Data Center World (October 2025, reported by *Data Center Knowledge*), and repeated at Data Center World April 2026 (per *Broadband Breakfast*, 2026-04-24).

## Why this claim cleared the gate

It is attributed to a named, verifiable head-of-state-level asserter, circulates independently in two trade outlets at two separate events, and is the load-bearing premise behind the Air Force RLP (offers due May 29, 2026), Stak Energy's North Slope lease application, and the state's pitch to hyperscalers. The steelman survives because stranded gas plus LNG monetization is a plausible future, not a hallucinated one. And it is rebuttable from primary evidence — utility statements on record, EIA rate data, the Air Force's own RLP text, and the Alaska LNG project's no-FID status.

## Steelman (in one paragraph)

Alaska has enormous proven stranded gas. If Alaska LNG reaches a Final Investment Decision and gets built, gas costs to Southcentral utilities could in theory fall enough to push industrial power into the single-digit cent range. The Governor is selling a future state to capital that thinks on 10-to-20-year infrastructure horizons, which is exactly the horizon a hyperscaler underwrites. Smart, informed people can believe this.

## The corrective thesis (one sentence)

Today's Alaska industrial power costs 14 to 17 cents per kilowatt-hour, the pipeline that would unlock the 4-to-5-cent number has no FID, and the utility that would serve the Air Force's chosen bases has stated on record it has no gas to serve a large new customer — so any pro forma underwritten on Dunleavy's headline rate is pricing a future that may not arrive on the schedule the deal needs.

## Evidence spine (load-bearing primary sources)

1. **Chugach Electric / Julie Hasquet (ADN, 2026-05-05):** "We currently do not have gas to serve a large data center."
2. **Air Force RLP language (April 2026):** JBER sites have "insufficient power" for a data center and will need a new grid connection.
3. **EIA 2024 industrial rate data (cited by Stan Jones, ADN 2026-04-12):** Alaska industrial rate 14-17¢/kWh; Virginia 8.99¢; Texas 6.12¢; Washington 6.61¢.
4. **Wood Mackenzie (cited in ADN 2026-04-12):** Alaska LNG Phase 1 gas projected at $10-13.70/MMBtu vs current Cook Inlet ~$8.69 — LNG-era gas would be *more* expensive than today's already-expensive supply.
5. **Hilcorp / Enstar (ADN 2026-04-28):** Hilcorp raising gas prices to Mat-Su utilities 49% to $11.75/Mcf by 2028. Enstar's John Sims called it "the start of an economy that just doesn't work anymore."
6. **Alaska Legislature (ADN 2026-02-11):** Senate Majority Leader Cathy Giessel called Glenfarne's Alaska LNG construction timeline "completely unrealistic."
7. **Cook Inlet supply collapse (ADN 2026-05-05):** Dropped from ~30% to less than 1% of Chugach's fuel mix. Chugach-Hilcorp contract expires 2028.

## Alaska industry consequence

Any operator underwriting a JBER, Eielson, or Clear lease bid on the Air Force RLP at 4-5¢/kWh power is underwriting a future that requires a $44-billion pipeline FID, a 5-to-10-year build, and gas supply at a price lower than today's already-rising rate. The capital allocation that follows from the wrong number will be subcontracted to firms with the patience to wait that out, not built locally.
