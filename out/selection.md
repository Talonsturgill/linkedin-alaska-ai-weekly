## Layers
- L1: DOE Defense Production Act Title III Authority (Secretary of Energy Chris Wright; 50 U.S.C. §4531)
- L2: DOE Grant Award — $89M federal / $190.4M total to Terra Energy Center Corp. (DOE OCED/HGEO)
- L3: West Susitna Access Road NEPA EIS — CHOKEPOINT (DOT&PF Commissioner Ryan Anderson; dot.alaska.gov/westsusitnaaccess/)
- L4: Matanuska Electric Association Binding PPA (MEA Board; currently nonbinding term sheet only)
- L5: Greenfield Coal Mine Permitting (ADNR, BLM AK, MSHA, ADEC — diffuse, parallel)

## Chokepoint
L3 — Alaska DOT&PF Commissioner Ryan Anderson's Record of Decision on the West Susitna Access Road EIS. Single-agency authority, binary decision: sign ROD (mine site accessible) or withhold (plant has no fuel supply). Estimated no earlier than late 2027. No other actor in the stack can substitute.

## Structural Read
The DOE grant announcement signals political will, not construction-readiness. The real option on the $190M+ investment stack is a 22-mile gravel road through 180+ stream crossings in a remote Alaska drainage — a road that has no formal EIS filing yet. Coal-for-AI in Alaska is a 5-year infrastructure buildout that runs through a state environmental review desk, not a federal grant office. Investors and program managers who follow the grant announcement headline face a 5+ year gap before baseload power is available.

## Forward Implication
Track the West Susitna Access Road EIS docket at dot.alaska.gov and the Army Corps Section 404 wetlands permit application — neither existed as formal filings as of June 2026. The DOE grant agreement execution (expected within 90 days of June 4) will contain feasibility-study milestones that reveal whether the timeline is credible. MEA board members face a 12-18 month decision window on converting the nonbinding term sheet to a binding PPA before the feasibility-study period expires.
