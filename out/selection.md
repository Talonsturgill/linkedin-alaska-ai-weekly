## Package angle
This week handed Alaska three procurement-style deadlines and one finished product. The deadlines (NSF AI hub June 16, the LNG Senate vote June 19, the Air Force data-center bids June 29) are all about hosting or enabling someone else's AI economy through land, power, grid, and training pipelines. The finished product, the UAF chinook model, is the only one where Alaska already owns the asset instead of negotiating to host it. The frame is ownership versus tenancy. Alaska's scarce convening capital is being spread across deadlines to rent out inputs, while the one homegrown, value-capturing AI build came out of a university lab on Navy tag data. The lead is the model. The tension is whether Alaska treats it as a one-off or as the template for which layer of the stack it should actually be building.
