## Package angle
This week Alaska's AI economy got shaped less by what the state did than by what it declined to do. Glenfarne signed a non-binding LOI to pipe North Slope gas to Chugach Electric, the Railbelt's anchor utility, and the Air Force's clock on commercial AI data-center leases at three Alaska bases kept running toward a June 29 deadline. The private build and the federal clock are moving. The state's own two instruments stalled in the same seven days. Dunleavy vetoed SB 258, the bill that would have barred agencies from single-vendor software lock-in, the exact terms on which public-sector AI tooling gets bought, and HB 381, the LNG tax framework the cheap-power pitch depends on, collapsed on the session's final day and slid into a second special session starting June 20. The through-line for the LinkedIn audience is a widening gap between private capital and federal clocks that keep moving and a public counterparty whose procurement and fiscal terms keep getting vetoed or deferred. The lead is the SB 258 veto, because procurement leverage is the quiet instrument that decides the price and lock-in of every enterprise-AI contract the state signs, and Alaska just gave that leverage back to the vendors.
