## Package angle
The most consequential AI-and-robotics moment in Alaska this week didn't happen on a contract sheet. It happened on a drop zone. RED FLAG-Alaska 26-2 wired uncrewed reconnaissance into a live multinational forcible-entry, the clearest sign yet that Alaska's terrain and airspace are the place the country proves its autonomous systems work in the hardest conditions. The same week, two more pieces of Alaska got volunteered as test bed. The Air Force wants developers to prove AI data centers can run on permafrost and a strained Interior grid by June 29, and GCI is proving multi-gigabit satellite middle-mile in four roadless hubs. Alaska is becoming the national stress-test environment for AI infrastructure and autonomy. The leverage in being the proving ground is real, the gatekeeper sets the standard. The risk is that the proving ground furnishes free R&D for firms headquartered Outside while the programs, the platforms, and the recurring revenue stay with them. The question the desk puts to the audience is whether being the test bed converts into Alaskan ownership or just subsidizes everyone else's.
