Fresh entity, never led. ## Package angle
This week the architecture for AI got drafted somewhere other than Alaska. The Senate Armed Services Committee advanced a $1.14 trillion defense bill that builds a brand-new four-star command to own the military's autonomy buy and writes the rules for autonomous weapons into statute. A bipartisan House draft wrote the first federal playbook for what employers owe workers when AI cuts jobs. And the one layer Alaska actually controls, its own connectivity, went dark again when a GCI subsea cable broke in the Aleutians and weather stalled the repair. The through-line for the LinkedIn audience is control. Alaska keeps showing up to the AI build-out as the operating environment, the affected employer, and the place the cable fails, not as the party holding the pen. The lead is the NDAA, because a consolidated autonomy command turns "we host the proving ground" into "do we hold a program node," and the only lever that changes that is delegation capital spent in conference before the FY2027 bill locks. The question the desk puts to the audience is whether Alaska's congressional leverage gets spent on wiring the state into the new command, or on the same MILCON line items as every year.
