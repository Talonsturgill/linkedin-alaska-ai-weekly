# VOL. 02 Selection — 2026-05-20

## Mode
**Deep Dive.** One frame dominates the week: Alaska's AI infrastructure architecture is being built piece by piece in plain sight, and the regulatory rails just got pulled before the train arrived. The Stak Energy preliminary lease is the sharpest in-window event with a hard clock (June 15 public comment close), it pairs naturally with the death of SB 250 the same week, and the rest of the lineup (Pikka's remote ops, Glenfarne / ConocoPhillips, HB 78 veto) reinforces the same structural argument without diffusing the lead. A Weekly Brief mode would let the lead breathe less. Deep Dive lets the post take a position.

## Package angle
On May 12, Alaska DNR opened a 60-day public comment window on a $500 million 3-gigawatt AI data center on the North Slope. On May 18 SB 250, the only Juneau vehicle that would have set ratepayer cost-allocation rules and community benefit agreements for data center power, was heard-and-held in House CRA and died at sine die on May 21. On May 19 the override of Dunleavy's HB 78 pension veto fell seven votes short, locking in the recruiting math the state agencies and federal contractors have to staff this with. The week's structural argument: Alaska is being asked to host an AI infrastructure economy without the statutory rails to price it, the workforce architecture to staff it, or a settled gas-supply story to power it. The capital is moving anyway. The state's institutional plumbing is not catching up on the same clock.

## Lineup

### LEAD — E1: Stak Energy $500M North Slope AI data center, DNR preliminary lease May 12, public comment closes June 15
- Anchorage-based Stak Energy proposes a one-square-mile, 50-year lease, 3 GW gas-powered AI/cloud compute campus ~25 miles south of major Slope infrastructure off the Dalton Highway.
- Power draw exceeds twice combined urban Alaska electric + heating fuel use.
- No firm gas supply contract disclosed; turbine supply chains backlogged seven years.
- 2028 initial operations target.
- Confidence: high. Primary sources: ADN, Northern Journal (Nathaniel Herz), DNR preliminary best-interest determination.
- New frame, not a re-lead on JBER / Eielson / Clear lease, not a re-lead on AK firms missing from defense AI procurement.

### SUPPORT 1 — D2: SB 250 data center cost-allocation bill dies in House CRA at session end
- Senate passed 14-5 on reconsideration May 16.
- House CRA heard-and-held May 18.
- Bill dies at sine die May 21.
- Would have required RCA-approved community benefit agreements and made data centers bear their own utility infrastructure costs.
- Confidence: high. Primary source: akleg.gov record.
- Pairs structurally with E1: a $500M private project arrived the same week Juneau killed the ground rules.

### SUPPORT 2 — C1: Pikka first oil with remote operations center (May 18)
- Santos 51% / Repsol 49%; first oil through LACT meter May 18.
- Remote operations center confirmed as a designed-in facility feature.
- Plateau target 80,000 bbl/day Q3 2026.
- Confidence: high (with softening on the "first greenfield ever" framing — use designed-in / centralized only).
- Pairs with E1 as the operational model of where Slope industry is going. Centralized remote ops is the same architectural pattern the AI compute campus would lean on.

### SUPPORT 3 — D1: Dunleavy vetoes HB 78; override fails 33 of 60 on May 19
- First defined-benefit pension legislation for AK public employees since 2006.
- Override needed 40; got 33; failed May 19.
- Confidence: high. Primary sources: ADN, APM.
- Pairs as the workforce side of the same equation. Whatever staffs Stak, the contractors, the utility, the regulator, gets recruited in a state that just signaled the retention deal is not coming.

### OPTIONAL SUPPORT 4 — B1: Glenfarne / ConocoPhillips 30-year gas supply (May 18)
- 30-year precedent agreement signed; four supply commitments now in hand.
- Phase One 739-mile, 42-inch pipeline targeting first gas 2029.
- Confidence: high.
- Inclusion decision: include only if the word budget allows. The gas-math context is the real story behind the Stak proposal — Stak's power draw is twice urban Alaska's combined electric and heat use, and the only realistic gas story for the Slope is Glenfarne's pipeline that doesn't deliver until 2029 at the earliest. A one-sentence aside is enough.
