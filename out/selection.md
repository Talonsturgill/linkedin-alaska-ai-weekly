# Anchorage Desk — Selection, 14 August 2026

## Subject
**Keith McCormick** — Anchorage Assembly Member (Eagle River / Chugiak district),
Anchorage Assembly, Municipality of Anchorage.

**ROLE (kicker middle slot): `MUNICIPAL`**

**Anchorage tie.** Elected member of the Anchorage Assembly representing the Eagle
River/Chugiak district within the Municipality of Anchorage. The decision governs an APD
surveillance-analytics system, the Real-Time Crime Center, deployed across the Anchorage bowl.

## The decision
**Date: 2026-08-04** (10 days before publication, comfortably inside the 60-day window)

At the August 4, 2026 Anchorage Assembly meeting, members declined to approve APD's
proposed ~$600,000 three-year contract with Thundercat Technology Inc. to upgrade the
Real-Time Crime Center, and postponed action to the August 18, 2026 meeting. McCormick led
the objection that the municipality has no code-level data-governance rules over what the
RTCC collects, who can access it, and how long it is retained.

**The binary he owned.** Fund the ~$600k three-year Thundercat RTCC analytics upgrade now,
versus withhold approval until Anchorage Municipal Code sets binding collection, access, and
retention guardrails.

**System scope.** The RTCC ingests up to 750 volunteer-registered camera feeds plus drones,
license-plate readers, and object-recognition / video-processing analytics. The Assembly
barred facial recognition in 2023.

## Structural read
The object of the contract is not an AI model, it is the algorithmic-analytics and
camera/ALPR/drone infrastructure that surveillance AI runs on. McCormick's objection targets
the missing legal architecture rather than the technology's merits, and that is the seam where
Alaska AI oversight tends to fall through. Anchorage barred facial recognition in 2023 but on
this record has no binding code governing the object-recognition and Flock-style analytics the
upgrade would expand. The countervailing case is real. APD frames this as routine public-safety
modernization, and a reasonable member could approve with committee notification as the check.
That is why it clears position-takeable rather than reading as advocacy.

## Forward implication
The binding vote lands at the **August 18, 2026** Assembly meeting, four days after publication.
Watch whether McCormick converts the objection into a code-level data-governance amendment as a
condition of approval, or whether the Assembly approves the Thundercat contract as-is and defers
governance to executive-branch policy. His prior no on the March 2026 data-center zoning
ordinance AO 2026-27 suggests he keeps pressing the guardrails-before-infrastructure line.
