# Selection — Received Wisdom NO. 09 (2026-06-05)

## The claim (verbatim)
"Congress is right now considering federal legislation that would override state laws protecting workers, kids and communities from the harmful impacts of AI."

- **Asserter:** LeiLauni Scheideman, President, Alaska Airlines Flight Attendants Council 30 (AFA-CWA)
- **Where:** Anchorage Daily News opinion, 1 June 2026
- **Slice:** ak_press
- **URL:** https://www.adn.com/opinions/2026/05/31/opinion-dont-let-washington-ground-alaskas-ability-to-protect-workers-from-ai/

## Steelman (state it at its strongest, fairly)
Federal preemption of state AI rules could chill state legislatures from passing ANY AI law, including worker and consumer protections, because the legal boundary between regulating AI "development" and "deployment" is not settled. Earlier moratorium drafts in this Congress did propose sweeping preemption of all state AI regulation, and the AFL-CIO has campaigned hard against federal preemption as a worker-protection threat. For a union representing flight attendants at a tech-adjacent employer, the risk reads as structural, not just textual. A federal floor can become a federal ceiling.

## One-sentence corrective thesis
The bill now on the table explicitly preserves — not erases — state authority over AI use and deployment where worker, child-safety, and consumer protections actually live; the real Alaska risk is legislative inertia, because Alaska has no enacted state AI law and a narrow window to pass one before a federal development-rule moratorium hardens.

## Evidence spine (primary)
1. **Roll Call, 4 June 2026** (Obernolte-Trahan bipartisan discussion draft): preempts only state laws "specifically regulating the development" of AI models for three years, and states "preemption would not apply to laws related to the use or deployment of AI models." That carve-out is exactly where worker / child-safety / consumer protections sit.
   - https://rollcall.com/2026/06/04/bipartisan-ai-draft-proposes-three-year-preemption-of-state-laws/
2. **Alaska State Legislature — HCR 3** (AI task-force resolution): still in House Finance Committee, last action 16 May 2025, no floor vote. Alaska has no enacted state AI law to preempt under any reading of the federal bill.
   - https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HCR3

## Fairness note for the writer
Scheideman's op-ed (June 1) predates the Obernolte-Trahan draft reported June 4. Frame the correction around the bill *now on the table*, not as her being careless. The generous read: she is right that preemption is live and worth fighting; the part the alarm leaves out is that this specific draft carves out the very protections she names, and that Alaska's bigger exposure is having nothing on its own books yet.

## AK business consequence
Alaska delegation's vote on the federal AI bill; whether Alaska employers and unions (Alaska Airlines, GCI, Alaska Communications) treat the federal posture as a threat to fight or a floor to build on; whether the Legislature revives HCR 3 or passes AI-in-the-workplace rules before the three-year federal window closes.
