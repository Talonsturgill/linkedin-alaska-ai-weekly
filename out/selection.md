## The decision
**Date: 11 June 2026.** GCI committed to using Starlink's dedicated service with bonded
gateways as a resiliency layer for the terrestrial fiber and microwave networks already
serving Bethel, Sitka, Kotzebue and Dillingham. If those existing systems go down, GCI
will use Starlink to carry business and residential traffic out of those communities.
Unalaska and Nome are already live on earlier deployments.

**The binary Wailand owned:** buy outage recovery from SpaceX's LEO bonded gateways, or
build additional GCI-owned terrestrial or GEO redundancy to the same four hubs.

## Structural read (desk position)
Defensible and low-regret on the merits. Bonded gateways buy multi-gigabit outage
recovery for exactly the hub communities where GCI-owned redundancy is slowest and
costliest to build, and GCI framed it as complementary to fiber and microwave rather
than a replacement. The honest tension is dependency concentration. Each incremental
commitment of critical middle-mile resiliency to one vendor narrows GCI's future
negotiating leverage and raises data-sovereignty questions for the health, education and
public-safety traffic riding that path.

The AI nexus is real but peripheral, and the post must say so. This is the data
infrastructure the AI era runs on, not an AI model, a compute-siting deal, or a power
purchase. Do not inflate it into an AI data-center story.

## Forward implication
Whether GCI extends bonded-gateway backup past these four hubs, and on what commercial
terms with SpaceX. Watch for a disclosed dollar figure, the duration of the Starlink
arrangement, and any middle-mile tariff or federal broadband (RDOF/BEAD) filing showing
how much of rural Alaska's transport resiliency GCI will route through a single LEO
provider.
