## Mode
**Deep Dive.** One in-window event carries a real structural tension. A GCI subsea fiber break severed internet and wireless to six Aleutian communities (Akutan, Chignik Bay, False Pass, King Cove, Perryville, Unalaska), dropping them onto satellite backup with full repair only expected by mid-June. That outage sits directly underneath the story Washington and developers keep telling about Alaska as the next AI-hosting frontier. The position the desk takes: capital is being marshaled for the top of the AI stack (compute, hosting) on a connectivity-and-power substrate that is both physically fragile and under federal subsidy review. That is a genuine false-frontier to argue, so Deep Dive over Weekly Brief.

## Package angle
Alaska keeps getting pitched as the AI-hosting frontier, and the week handed us a clean rebuttal. On June 1 GCI confirmed a subsea fiber break had cut six Aleutian communities to satellite backup, with full fiber repair not expected until roughly mid-June. The compute-frontier pitch (DAF's roughly 4,700-acre AI data-center lease offer across JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS, proposals closed May 29, and a $500M private North Slope data-center proposal with no disclosed investors) all assume a connectivity-and-power substrate that just failed in the Aleutians, with Chugach Electric on record that it has no spare gas for a large data center. Underneath sits the verified capital story: a May ProPublica and Anchorage Daily News investigation found Alaska telecoms drew $4.6 billion in Universal Service Fund subsidies since 2016, more than $600 per resident per year and the most of any state, while Alaska's broadband clocked the slowest in the country, and the FCC is now circulating a plan to overhaul the Alaska Plan. GCI is simultaneously buying Quintillion at a $310M enterprise value and carrying $121.2M in BEAD obligations to extend the same fragile subsea fiber. The frame for the LinkedIn audience: the AI-frontier thesis prices the glamorous top of the stack and ignores the rail at the bottom, and the rail is both breaking and losing its subsidy backstop.
