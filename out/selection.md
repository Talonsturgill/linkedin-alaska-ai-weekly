## Package angle
This week the most important Alaska AI news wasn't AI. It was the substrate underneath it. Alaska Communications, the state's only undersea-fiber carrier, changed CEOs mid-BEAD buildout, handing the backbone every future Alaska data center and AI workload depends on to a fiber-build specialist effective September 1. Alaska Airlines finished wiring its entire regional E175 fleet with Starlink, turning each intrastate flight into a 500 Mbps node over communities with no terrestrial gigabit. And the Air Force's commercial AI data-center lease clock ran down to June 29 at JBER, Eielson, and Clear, the looming demand all that connectivity would have to carry. Three moves, all in the physical layer, none in the application layer. Not one Alaska AI deployment, contract, or pilot landed in the window, and the closest deployed Alaska-built AI tool, NANA's workforce platform, launched back in May. Meanwhile Governor Dunleavy vetoed $89.5 million from the FY27 budget, including expansions to the vocational and technical-training programs that would supply the people to build and run all of it. For capital allocators and program managers the read is blunt. In Alaska right now the money and the buildout are going into pipes and land, the human pipeline is being trimmed, and the AI itself is still mostly somewhere else.
