# VOL. 05 Selection — Alaska.Ai LinkedIn — 2026-06-11

## Mode
**Deep Dive.** One live, in-window procurement event carries a real structural tension. On June 5 the Air Force released RFLP AFCEC-26-R-0006-O offering parcels at JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS for privately financed, privately operated AI data centers, proposals due June 29. The same week two federal moves point in opposite directions for Alaska's share of the AI buildout, and the contrast is a position worth taking, so Deep Dive over Weekly Brief.

## Package angle (the position)
Alaska is being offered the **landlord** seat in the federal AI buildout, not the **operator** seat. The DAF RFLP hands all financing and operation to a private developer on leases up to 50 years; the state and its firms collect land rent, construction, and grid offtake, while the AI workloads (and the recurring contract revenue) belong to whoever operates them. Two June moves reshuffled the operator seat at the exact moment Alaska is deciding how to show up. NSPM-11 (June 5) orders DoD and the intel community to break AI vendor lock-in, onboard more and smaller commercial vendors, and reset procurement inside 120 days, which is the door post_001 said Alaska firms couldn't walk through, now being pried open federally. And the SBA's June 11 proposed rule entrenches the one lane Alaska reliably wins, the ANC entity-owned 8(a) preference, while raising the bar for everyone else. So the structural choice in front of Alaska is sharp. Compete for the operator seat that NSPM-11 is widening, or keep banking on the landlord-and-preference model the SBA rule just reinforced. The lead is the June 29 DAF deadline; the engine is NSPM-11; the lane is the 8(a) carve-out; Terra's $89M coal grant is the power leg the landlord model still can't pencil.

The landlord-vs-operator tension is new to the desk.
