## Chokepoint
Layer 3. **18 CFR 375.308(b)(1)** delegates to the Director of FERC's Office of Energy Projects, or
the Director's designee, authority to take appropriate action on **uncontested** applications for
amendments to Part I Federal Power Act licenses.

Binary: if no protest or motion to intervene is on file at 5:00 p.m. ET on **2026-08-28**, the
application is uncontested and one delegated officer may act on it. If it is contested, that
officer's delegated authority does not reach it.

Asymmetric because it is one officer, one binary, inside their own delegated authority, and the
switch that arms or disarms that authority is a clock rather than a vote. The BPMC, by contrast, is
a six-member governance body, not a chokepoint.

## Structural read
The nameplate arithmetic is the trick. The amendment would raise average annual generation by about
38 percent while adding only 0.3 MW of nameplate, so it rides the non-capacity amendment track
rather than a relicensing, and non-capacity amendments are exactly the class 18 CFR 375.308(b) hands
to a single delegated officer when nobody contests them. The largest single addition of firm non-gas
Railbelt energy in the queue is, procedurally, a routine amendment. Not a scandal, just the ordinary
shape of Federal Power Act administration, which is why leverage sits in a clock and not a hearing.
Honest limits: AEA's own page says the funding source is undetermined and puts first power at the far
end of a six-year study-permit-build sequence. This does nothing for the Cook Inlet gas constraint
binding Railbelt interconnection today.
