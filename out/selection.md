## Package angle
The Alaska data-center story has been told as a power-supply story all month, cheap North Slope gas, the LNG tax fight, Cook Inlet math. This week the binding constraint moved somewhere else. On June 18 FERC told the six big organized markets to fix the rules for connecting large AI loads to the grid, an admission that data-center demand has outrun the existing interconnection process. Alaska's Railbelt is in none of those markets, so none of that federal repair reaches it, and the Regulatory Commission of Alaska is the only body that could write an equivalent large-load rule for the state. It hasn't. Meanwhile the demand is already at the door. DNR just closed its comment window on STAK Energy's North Slope gas-powered AI campus and is reviewing toward a final lease decision, and the Air Force's commercial AI data-center proposals for JBER, Eielson, and Clear SFS are due June 29. Federal money is landing too, the FY2027 NDAA advanced June 23 with roughly $2.6 billion in Alaska defense construction including the Eielson range and the Kodiak spaceport. So the capital and the load are arriving on schedule while the one administrative artifact that decides who connects, in what order, and on whose dime does not exist. For anyone underwriting or siting compute in Alaska, the diligence question isn't whether the power is cheap. It's whether there's a rulebook to plug into at all.
