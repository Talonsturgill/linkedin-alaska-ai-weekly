## Mode
**Deep Dive.** One genuinely new in-window capital event carries a real structural tension: on June 4 the DOE awarded $89M to a speculative Alaska coal plant pitched to power AI data centers, even as the AI work actually landing in Alaska this week needs zero new megawatts. That false-binary (gigawatt buildout vs. deployable AI that runs on people, data, and software) is a position worth taking, so Deep Dive over Weekly Brief.

## Package angle
Washington just put $89 million behind an Alaska coal plant to feed AI data centers, and it is chasing the wrong layer of the stack. Terra Energy Center Corp., an Anchorage affiliate of Flatlands Energy, was selected June 4 for a DOE grant to study a 1,250-megawatt coal-fired plant with carbon capture in the Susitna watershed, part of a roughly $700M national package that Latitude Media reports is explicitly designed to power data centers. The plant has no permitted mine, no finalized design, and no signed PPA, which means no electrons before the 2030s. Meanwhile the AI dollars that pencil right now in Alaska need no new generation at all: NSF's $250M SBIR restart opened a July 27 clock for deep-tech seed capital, HB 117 went to the governor June 1 extending the state into AI computer-vision trawl-monitoring data, and the $272M Rural Health Transformation portal (June 22 deadline) funds AI clinical tools and drone delivery. The frame for the LinkedIn audience: the binding constraint on the AI work shipping in Alaska this decade is clearances, data, compliance, and workforce, not megawatts, and an $89M coal study answers a bottleneck that the near-term money does not have. The rulebook is moving the same way the money is, outside Alaska: a June 4 House discussion draft (the Great American AI Act) would preempt state AI-development law for three years.

Lead frame is NEW. It does not re-lead the last four weeks: AI governance vacuum (06-03), data-center grid economics / GVEA fuel charge (06-02), federal preemption of workplace AI (06-01), autonomy-vs-fragile-rails (05-31). This extends the power-math thread with a genuinely new federal capital event and the opposite tension (Washington IS now funding electrons, and it's still the wrong layer), rather than restating the 06-02 grid-price argument.
